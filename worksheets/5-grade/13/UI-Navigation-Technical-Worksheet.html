Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A user interface (UI) is what you see and use on a screen: icons, menus, buttons, and windows. UI navigation means moving through these parts to complete tasks.

Do this:

  1. Name one app you use and one UI element in it (for example, a button).
  2. Circle the part of a UI you click to perform an action: Button · Wallpaper

Part 2 – Parts of a UI (about 5 minutes)

Common UI elements are icons (small pictures), menus (lists of options), buttons, toolbars, and windows. They are designed to be recognizable.

Real-life scenario: In a drawing app, Paula clicks the pencil icon on the toolbar to start drawing.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why do icons use pictures instead of words?

Part 3 – Navigation patterns (about 5 minutes)

Many apps share patterns: a menu bar at the top, tabs to switch sections, breadcrumbs that show where you are, and a search bar.

Real-life scenario: On a school portal, Eli sees Home > Grades > Math in the breadcrumbs.

Critical thinking: How do breadcrumbs help you?

Word bank: Tabs · Menu bar · Breadcrumbs · Search bar

Part 4 – Finding features (about 5 minutes)

If you do not see a feature, check the menu, right-click, or use the Help option. Exploring carefully helps you learn a new UI.

Real-life scenario: Zoe cannot find Print. She opens the File menu and sees it.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What steps would you follow to find a feature?

Do this:

  1. List three places to look for a feature.
  2. Circle the best approach: Explore menus step by step · Click everything very fast

Part 5 – UI sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a simple app screen with a menu bar, a toolbar, and a button. Label each part.
