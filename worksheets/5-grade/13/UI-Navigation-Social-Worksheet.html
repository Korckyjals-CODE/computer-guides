UI Navigation: Social Worksheet

Grade 5 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Interfaces are made by people for people. A good UI should be understandable by users of different ages, languages, and abilities. Everyone deserves an interface they can use.

Do this:

  1. Write one app that is easy for a younger child to use, and say why.
  2. Circle the idea that is true: Good interfaces should help everyone · Only experts need clear interfaces

Part 2 – Who uses the interface? (about 5 minutes)

Children, older adults, and people with disabilities may all use the same app. Designers think about all of them.

Real-life scenario: A grandmother cannot read tiny text on a banking app. A larger font helps her.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What might be hard about an interface for someone new to technology?

Do this: Circle Helps or Hurts.

Part 3 – Language and symbols (about 5 minutes)

Icons and words can mean different things in different places. Labels, translations, and clear symbols help everyone.

Real-life scenario: A visitor from another country does not understand an icon. A text label solves it.

Critical thinking: Why add words to icons?

Word bank: Label · Translation · Clear symbol

Do this: Fill in the best fix.

ProblemFix
Icon is confusing
User speaks another language
User cannot see colors well

Part 4 – Giving feedback (about 5 minutes)

Users can tell designers what is confusing. Respectful, specific feedback helps improve a UI for all.

Real-life scenario: A student writes: “I could not find the Save button because it is hidden in a menu.”

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is specific feedback better than “It is bad”?

Do this:

  1. Write one helpful feedback sentence about an app.
  2. Circle the helpful feedback: The button label is unclear · This app is dumb

Part 5 – Interface for all (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw one change you would make so that everyone can use an app.

Write who benefits from your change.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know interfaces serve many kinds of users.
  • □ I know why labels help.
  • □ I can give respectful feedback.
  • □ I can name a feature that helps accessibility.
  • □ I can suggest one UI improvement.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
