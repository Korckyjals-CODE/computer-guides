## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** Interfaces are made by people for people. A good UI should be understandable by users of different ages, languages, and abilities. Everyone deserves an interface they can use.

## Part 2 – Who uses the interface? (about 5 minutes)

Children, older adults, and people with disabilities may all use the same app. Designers think about all of them.

**Real-life scenario:** A grandmother cannot read tiny text on a banking app. A larger font helps her.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What might be hard about an interface for someone new to technology?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Helps** or **Hurts**.

| Feature | Effect on users |
|---|---|
| Large, clear buttons | Helps · Hurts |
| Tiny gray text | Helps · Hurts |
| Icons with labels | Helps · Hurts |
| Many confusing menus | Helps · Hurts |

## Part 3 – Language and symbols (about 5 minutes)

Icons and words can mean different things in different places. Labels, translations, and clear symbols help everyone.

**Real-life scenario:** A visitor from another country does not understand an icon. A text label solves it.

**Critical thinking:** Why add words to icons?

## Part 4 – Giving feedback (about 5 minutes)

Users can tell designers what is confusing. Respectful, specific feedback helps improve a UI for all.

**Real-life scenario:** A student writes: “I could not find the Save button because it is hidden in a menu.”

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why is specific feedback better than “It is bad”?
