Efficiency: Social Worksheet

Grade 5 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Group efficiency is about fair roles, clear communication, and not wasting teammates’ time—without pressuring people unfairly.

Do this:

  1. Name a group project that needed computer time.
  2. Circle a fair efficiency idea: Clear roles · One person does everything secretly

Part 2 – Roles beat chaos (about 5 minutes)

When everyone edits the same sentence at once, work collides. Assign who types, who researches, who checks.

Real-life scenario: Four students type in one shared cell. Sentences overwrite each other. Ten minutes vanish.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How would roles make that time more efficient?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 3 – Respect others’ focus (about 5 minutes)

Poking a focused teammate every thirty seconds with memes looks funny but destroys efficiency and kindness.

Real-life scenario: During a timed research block, Jordan keeps spinning a classmate’s chair and asking off-topic questions.

Critical thinking: What fair signal could the class use for “focus time / talk time”?

Do this:

  1. Write one respectful way to ask for help without breaking focus.
  2. Circle better timing: Wait for a check-in · Interrupt mid-sentence every time

Part 4 – Efficiency without exclusion (about 5 minutes)

The fastest typist should not shut others out. Teaching and rotating roles builds a stronger team than one “efficient” monopolizer.

Real-life scenario: Only Sam knows the shortcuts. Sam refuses to teach “because it’s faster if I do it.” Others learn nothing.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is teaching a teammate efficient for the long term?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 5 – Team efficiency charter (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Create a 3-rule charter for efficient, kind group computer work.

Underline the rule that protects quieter members.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know group efficiency needs clear roles.
  • □ I respect others’ focus time.
  • □ I avoid excluding teammates for short-term speed.
  • □ I can propose fair check-ins.
  • □ I can write a team charter.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
