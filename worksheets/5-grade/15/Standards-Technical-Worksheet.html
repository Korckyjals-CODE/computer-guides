Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A standard is a rule that many people agree to follow. File naming standards make it easier to share, sort, and search files across a whole group.

Do this:

  1. Name one standard you use every day, such as a traffic signal.
  2. Circle the purpose of a standard: Everyone does it the same way · Everyone does it differently

Part 2 – What is a standard? (about 5 minutes)

A standard describes the same way to do something. Examples: USB ports, the A4 paper size, and file names like Report-Math-2026-11-30.

Real-life scenario: A school uses three different naming styles. Searching is slow.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How would a standard help?

Do this: Circle Standard or Not a standard.

Part 3 – Parts of a naming standard (about 5 minutes)

A standard often has parts: Subject – Topic – Date – Version. Example: Science-Plants-2026-11-30-v1.

Real-life scenario: Mia names her file “plants thing”. The standard says to follow the four-part format.

Critical thinking: Rewrite her name.

Part 4 – Why dates go year first (about 5 minutes)

2026-11-30 sorts in the right order. A format such as 30-11-2026 mixes months and years in lists.

Real-life scenario: A folder of files sorted by name shows December before November.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How could the date format help?

Do this:

  1. Write today's date in the format 2026-MM-DD.
  2. Circle the date format that sorts well: 2026-11-30 · 30-11-2026

Explain how the standard helps.
