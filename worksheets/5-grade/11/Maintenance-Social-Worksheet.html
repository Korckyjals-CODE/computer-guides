Maintenance: Social Worksheet

Grade 5 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Shared devices stay healthy when people share responsibility. Maintenance is not only an adult job—students can report problems, leave devices ready, and follow fair class rules. In this worksheet you will practice teamwork around care.

Do this:

  1. Name a shared device your class uses.
  2. Circle who should help keep it working: Only the teacher · Only IT · Students and adults together

Part 2 – Shared responsibility (about 5 minutes)

When many people use one laptop cart or lab, each person leaves the next person a working tool. That is shared maintenance.

Real-life scenario: After club time, three tablets are left with 5% battery and sticky screens. Morning class cannot start on time. Nobody meant harm—they just assumed someone else would plug them in.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What end-of-use habit would help the morning class?

Do this: Mark Helps the group or Hurts the group.

Action after using a shared tabletHelps or Hurts?
Return it to the cart and plug in the charger
Leave crumbs on the keyboard for the next student
Tell the teacher a case zipper is broken

Part 3 – Report early, without fear (about 5 minutes)

Small problems grow if people hide them. Reporting early is part of care. You do not have to “own” the blame to speak up.

Real-life scenario: Jordan notices a cracked charger tip on laptop #8. Jordan did not break it, but worries that speaking up will look like admitting fault.

Critical thinking: Write one respectful sentence Jordan could say to a teacher.

Do this: Circle the better choice.

What you noticeBetter choice
A fan is much louder than last weekReport it · Ignore it
Water near a keyboard (you did not spill it)Tell an adult · Walk away quietly
A password dialog looks unusualAsk a trusted adult · Type the password anyway

Part 4 – Fair class maintenance rules (about 5 minutes)

Fair rules say who cleans, who plugs in, and how long a turn lasts. Rules work best when students help write them.

Real-life scenario: Two students argue over the only working mouse. One keeps it the whole period. The other cannot finish a design task.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Write one fair rule for sharing and caring for lab tools.

Do this: Number these end-of-class steps 1, 2, and 3.

StepOrder (1, 2, or 3)
Push in chairs and leave the space tidy
Save work and log out or close apps as instructed
Return devices to the cart and check chargers

Part 5 – Maintenance team poster (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Design a class poster with three maintenance habits (report, recharge, tidy, update reminder).
  1. Write who the poster helps.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I see maintenance as a shared responsibility.
  • □ I can name habits that help the next person using a device.
  • □ I know to report early without hiding problems.
  • □ I can suggest a fair rule for class care.
  • □ I can design a short maintenance poster.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
