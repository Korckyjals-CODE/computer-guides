Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Maintenance means the regular care that keeps technology working—cleaning, checking cables, updating software, freeing storage, and fixing small issues early. In this worksheet you will name maintenance tasks, sort them by type, and plan a simple schedule.

Do this:

  1. Name one device at school or home that needs regular care.
  2. Write one maintenance task you already know for that device.

Part 2 – What maintenance includes (about 5 minutes)

Maintenance can be physical (dust, cables, cases) or digital (updates, backups, clearing space). Both kinds keep a system reliable. Skipping either kind can cause slowdowns or sudden failures.

Real-life scenario: A class laptop feels hot and shuts down during videos. The vents are packed with dust. Another laptop is slow because the storage is almost full. Both need maintenance—but different kinds.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which laptop needs physical care? Which needs digital care? Could one laptop need both?

Part 3 – Signs that maintenance is needed (about 5 minutes)

Warning signs include unusual heat, strange noises, low-storage messages, slow apps, and error pop-ups. Noticing signs early is part of good maintenance.

Real-life scenario: For two weeks a classroom desktop shows “storage almost full.” Students keep downloading large videos. On presentation day, the computer will not save the slides.

Critical thinking: What maintenance step could have prevented this? Write one sentence.

Do this: Match each sign with a likely maintenance action from the word bank.

Word bank: Clear unused files · Check vents / cooling · Update or reinstall the app · Inspect / replace cable

Part 4 – A simple maintenance schedule (about 5 minutes)

A schedule turns care into a habit: some tasks weekly, some monthly. Waiting until something breaks is reactive; a schedule is planned maintenance.

Real-life scenario: The school laptop cart has a posted checklist: Friday = plug in all devices; monthly = wipe screens and check cases; each term = ask IT about updates.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why might a posted schedule help more than “clean it when it feels dirty”?
