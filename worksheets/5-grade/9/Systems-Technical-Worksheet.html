Systems: Technical Worksheet

Grade 5 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A system is a set of parts that work together for one purpose. In this worksheet you will learn input, process, and output, see what happens when one part is missing, and include people as parts of a system.

Do this:

  1. Name one system you use (a bike, a lunch line, a game, a class screen—any example).
  2. In one sentence, what is that system for?

Part 2 – What goes in, what happens, what comes out (about 5 minutes)

Input is what goes into the system. The process is the work the parts do with that input. The output is what comes out. A bike is a system. Pushing the pedals is input. The chain turns the wheel: that is the process. The bike moving forward is the output. If you only look at one part, you miss how the system works.

Real-life scenario: You drop bread in a toaster and push the lever. A few minutes later, warm toast pops up. The toaster did not invent the job. The parts followed a path from bread to toast.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What is the input, the process, and the output? What output would you get if the heating part never turned on?

Do this: Fill in the missing stage.

System Input Process Output
Toaster Bread and a push of the lever Warm toast pops up
Electric pencil sharpener A pencil and a press of the button The blade turns
Alarm clock A time you set the night before A sound at that time

Part 3 – When one part is missing (about 5 minutes)

Parts depend on each other. A system can look fine and still fail because one link is gone. Finding the missing part is more useful than saying “the whole thing is broken.”

Real-life scenario: The class wants a video on the big screen. The computer is on. The projector light is on. The picture never appears. One cable between the computer and the projector is unplugged.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which part failed? Why would replacing the computer not fix this?

Critical thinking: Name one other school system that fails when a single part is missing. Say which part.

Do this: Write which part is missing or failed.

What you notice Which part failed?
Computer is on, projector is on, no picture
Bike pedals turn, but the back wheel does not
Alarm is set, but you hear nothing because the speaker is off
Lunch line has food and trays, but no one knows the choices

Part 4 – People are parts too (about 5 minutes)

A system is not only machines. A person can be the part that decides, carries, or checks. Information, such as a list or a sign, can be a part too. If you leave the person out of the drawing, the system looks like it runs itself when it does not.

Real-life scenario: The lunch line has trays, a server, and students. The menu list for today is missing. Students reach the front and do not know what they can choose. The trays did not fail. The information part did.

Part 5 – Draw a system you know (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a system with at least three parts. Use arrows to show the order. Label the input and the output.
  1. Write one sentence. What is the purpose of your system?

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can explain a system as parts that work together for a purpose.
  • □ I can name input, process, and output for a real example.
  • □ I can find which part failed when the rest of a system still looks fine.
  • □ I can label a person, a tool, and information as parts.
  • □ I can draw a small system with arrows, an input, and an output.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
