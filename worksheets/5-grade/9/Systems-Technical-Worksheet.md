## Part 2 – What goes in, what happens, what comes out (about 5 minutes)

**Input** is what goes into the system. The **process** is the work the parts do with that input. The **output** is what comes out. A bike is a system. Pushing the pedals is input. The chain turns the wheel: that is the process. The bike moving forward is the output. If you only look at one part, you miss how the system works.

**Real-life scenario:** You drop bread in a toaster and push the lever. A few minutes later, warm toast pops up. The toaster did not invent the job. The parts followed a path from bread to toast.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What is the input, the process, and the output? What output would you get if the heating part never turned on?

## Part 3 – When one part is missing (about 5 minutes)

Parts **depend** on each other. A system can look fine and still fail because one link is gone. Finding the missing part is more useful than saying “the whole thing is broken.”

**Real-life scenario:** The class wants a video on the big screen. The computer is on. The projector light is on. The picture never appears. One cable between the computer and the projector is unplugged.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Which part failed? Why would replacing the computer not fix this?

## Part 4 – People are parts too (about 5 minutes)

A system is not only machines. A **person** can be the part that decides, carries, or checks. **Information**, such as a list or a sign, can be a part too. If you leave the person out of the drawing, the system looks like it runs itself when it does not.

**Real-life scenario:** The lunch line has trays, a server, and students. The menu list for today is missing. Students reach the front and do not know what they can choose. The trays did not fail. The information part did.
