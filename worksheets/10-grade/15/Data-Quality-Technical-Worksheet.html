Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Data quality describes how well data fits its purpose. Key dimensions are accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity and uniqueness. Poor quality leads to poor decisions.

Do this:

  1. Name one decision that depends on reliable data.
  2. Circle the data quality dimension for missing values: Completeness · Timeliness

Part 2 – Dimensions of quality (about 5 minutes)

Accuracy: values are correct. Completeness: nothing missing. Consistency: same format everywhere. Timeliness: up to date. Validity: follows rules. Uniqueness: no duplicates.

Real-life scenario: A table lists age = -5, a blank email and the same student twice.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which dimensions fail?

Part 3 – Finding errors (about 5 minutes)

Profiling counts blanks, duplicates and unusual values. Validation rules such as “age between 3 and 100” stop bad data at entry.

Real-life scenario: A form accepts the date 31 February.

Critical thinking: What validation rule would prevent this?

Word bank: rule · range · format

Part 4 – Cleaning data (about 5 minutes)

Cleaning includes removing duplicates, fixing formats, filling gaps and documenting changes. Keep the original data.

Real-life scenario: A column has USA, U.S.A. and United States.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How would you standardize it?

Do this:

  1. Write one cleaning step and why it is safe.
  2. Circle the safer practice: Keep a copy of the raw data · Overwrite without backup

Explain which one needs attention first.
