## Part 2 – Shared systems vs personal systems (about 5 minutes)

Teams need a **shared** source of truth (board, doc, calendar). Personal systems can differ if handoffs into the shared system are reliable.

**Real-life scenario:** One member tracks only in their head. Others use the board. The head-tracker’s tasks stay invisible until crisis.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What minimum shared update is fair to require?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

|---|---|
| Post status on the shared board twice a week | Yes · No |
| Refuse any shared tracker as “not my style” | Yes · No |
| Allow personal tools if mirrored to shared list | Yes · No |

## Part 3 – Notification culture (about 5 minutes)

Always-on pings feel productive but fracture deep work. Agree on response windows so productivity systems do not become surveillance.

**Real-life scenario:** A group chat expects replies within 2 minutes at 11 p.m. Members lose sleep and still produce weaker work.

**Critical thinking:** Propose a response-time agreement that protects rest.

## Part 4 – Hustle myth and equity (about 5 minutes)

Glorifying overwork hides unequal loads and access to quiet time/devices. Ethical productivity includes capacity and fairness.

**Real-life scenario:** The team praises the member who works until 2 a.m. because they alone have a private laptop and quiet room.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How can recognition focus on sustainable contribution?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Recognition style | Equitable? |
|---|---|
| Praise clear daytime progress updates | Yes · No |
| Only praise overnight grind | Yes · No |
| Redistribute tasks when one person is overloaded | Yes · No |
