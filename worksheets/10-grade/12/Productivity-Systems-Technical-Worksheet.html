Productivity Systems: Technical Worksheet

Grade 10 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A productivity system is a repeatable set of tools and rules for capturing tasks, prioritizing, scheduling, and reviewing—so work moves from brain → trusted list → done.

Do this:

  1. Name tools you already use (calendar, notes, to-do app, paper…).
  2. Circle a system trait: Capture + review rhythm · Only sticky notes forever with no review

Part 2 – Capture and clarify (about 5 minutes)

Capture everything that needs action into one inbox. Clarify each item: Is it actionable? What’s the next physical step? What’s done looks like?

Real-life scenario: Jay keeps tasks in chat, email, head, and three apps. Deadlines slip because nothing is complete or reviewed.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why does a single capture inbox reduce missed work?

Do this: Turn vague items into next actions.

ItemNext action (write one)
“Science project”
“Email coach”
“Study”

Part 3 – Prioritization frameworks (about 5 minutes)

Simple systems: Must / Should / Could, due-date sorting, or Eisenhower-style urgent vs important. Pick one and apply it consistently.

Real-life scenario: Before exams, Lea answers every chat instantly (urgent-feeling) and skips the study guide (important).

Critical thinking: How would an urgent/important split change Lea’s evening?

Do this:

  1. List 4 real tasks. Tag each Must/Should/Could.
  2. Circle a rule you will try for a week: Morning Must-list of 3 · No prioritization

Part 4 – Review loops (about 5 minutes)

Systems fail without a review: daily peek at Must items; weekly clean of inbox and calendar. Tools do not replace the review habit.

Real-life scenario: A fancy app fills with 200 unchecked tasks. Without weekly review it becomes guilt, not guidance.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What would a 15-minute weekly review include?

Part 5 – Design your system map (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Diagram your productivity system: Capture → Lists → Calendar → Review. Name the concrete tools.

Identify one weak link you will fix this week.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can define a productivity system.
  • □ I turn vague work into next actions.
  • □ I can prioritize with a simple framework.
  • □ I understand why reviews matter.
  • □ I can map my personal system.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
