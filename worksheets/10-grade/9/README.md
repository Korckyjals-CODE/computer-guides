# Impact – Worksheets (Grade 10, Week 9)

## For the teacher

- **Use:** Use these worksheets **whenever** the topic of impact fits your curriculum, especially the effects of automated services. They are self-contained learning activities, not tied to a specific computer project.
- **Format:** Students work **offline**, with **pen and paper** (and color pencils if you like). No computer or internet needed.
- **Time:** About **25 minutes** per worksheet. Each part has a suggested time (roughly 3–6 minutes) so students can pace themselves.
- **Guidance:** The text is written so that students can complete it **on their own**; no teacher guidance is required during the activity.
- **Standalone value:** Each worksheet teaches on its own. No computer project is required; optional hands-on activities may complement them if you choose.

## Learning objectives

### Technical worksheet

- Distinguish **intended** impact from **unintended** impact in an automated checkout system.
- Explain why an efficiency number does not show whether people who need help are better off.
- Read a **metric** for what it shows and what harm it can hide.
- Separate functions that must work locally from reports that can wait when a shared network fails.
- Sketch a fragile dependency and state a design rule that survives it.

### Social worksheet

- Show that a better average can hide who is shut out of a public service.
- Compare benefits and barriers for the agency, routine users, people without the required tool, and the worker whose role changed.
- Explain how automation moves work to exceptions, maintenance, and explanations.
- Decide who should control retention and sale of data from a system people must use.
- Write a recommendation that keeps a benefit, sets a limit, and leaves a person available when the system fails.
