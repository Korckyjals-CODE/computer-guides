Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Failure analysis is a structured way to understand why a system stopped working as expected. You separate symptoms from causes, isolate variables, use error clues, and document findings so the same failure is less likely to repeat.

Do this:

  1. Describe one failure you have seen (app crash, Wi-Fi drop, printer jam, login error).
  2. In a few words, what was the symptom people noticed first?

Part 2 – Symptoms vs causes (about 5 minutes)

A symptom is what users observe (slow, blank screen, error code). A cause is the underlying condition (full disk, faulty cable, buggy update, misconfiguration). Treating only symptoms (rebooting forever) without finding causes invites repeats.

Real-life scenario: Students say “the projector is broken.” The symptom is no image. Possible causes: sleep mode, loose HDMI, wrong input source, dead bulb, or laptop output disabled.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is “the projector is broken” too vague for analysis?

Do this: Mark each statement S (symptom) or C (possible cause).

Part 3 – Isolate and gather clues (about 5 minutes)

Good analysis isolates factors: Does it happen on one device or many? One account or all? Wired and wireless? After a change (update, new cable)? Capture error messages, times, and recent changes. Guessing without evidence wastes effort.

Real-life scenario: Only Room 12 cannot print. Rooms 11 and 13 work. The shared printer is online. Someone swapped Room 12's network cable yesterday during a desk move.

Critical thinking: Which clue most strongly points you toward the cable or local connection first? Why?


Do this: List three questions you would ask to isolate a “Wi-Fi keeps dropping” report.

Part 4 – Hardware, software, and documentation (about 5 minutes)

Failures may be hardware (physical parts), software (apps/OS), configuration, or human process (wrong steps). Writing a short incident note—what happened, evidence, likely cause, fix, prevention—turns a one-off repair into organizational learning.

Real-life scenario: A club laptop overheats and shuts down. Dust blocks the vents (hardware/environment). Fans spin at 100%. After cleaning, the team also writes: “Monthly vent check; don't block side vents with binders.”

Do this: Classify each failure mainly as Hardware, Software, or Process (best fit).

Part 5 – Analysis flow sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a flow: Observe symptom → Gather clues → Form hypothesis → Test/isolate → Fix → Document prevention.
  1. Star the step teams most often skip, and say why skipping it is costly.

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.
