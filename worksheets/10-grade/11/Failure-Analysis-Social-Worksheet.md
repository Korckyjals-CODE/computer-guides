## Part 2 – Blame culture vs learning culture (about 5 minutes)

A **blame culture** hunts for a person to punish. A **learning culture** asks what in the system (tools, training, time, process) allowed the failure—and how to improve. Blame often hides information; learning surfaces it.

**Real-life scenario:** A shared drive folder is deleted. Leadership demands “Who did it?” before asking whether recycle bin/versioning was enabled, who had delete rights, or whether a checklist existed for archive moves.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How might fear of blame stop someone from reporting an early mistake?

## Part 3 – Communicating during downtime (about 5 minutes)

Clear status updates reduce rumor. Good messages state **what is down**, **who is affected**, **what is known**, and **when the next update** will arrive—even if the cause is still unknown.

**Real-life scenario:** The grading portal fails before a deadline. Silence for three hours. Students panic-email teachers; rumors say “grades were wiped.” Actually only login is down; data is intact.

**Critical thinking:** Draft a 2–3 sentence status update that is honest without blaming anyone.

## Part 4 – Impact, equity, and ethical reporting (about 5 minutes)

Failures hit people unevenly: students with less home internet, one shared family device, or tight deadlines suffer more. **Ethical reporting** means sharing accurate facts, not exaggerating for drama or hiding to protect image.

**Real-life scenario:** Wi-Fi fails during a timed online quiz. Some students finish on mobile data; others cannot. A fair response may extend time or offer an alternate format—not insist “everyone had the same chance.”
