## Part 2 – Speed vs. access (about 5 minutes)

Removing text and menus can make a screen faster but harder for new users or screen readers.

**Real-life scenario:** An app hides labels to look clean. Blind users cannot tell what buttons do.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How can designers keep both speed and access?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Keeps access** or **Hurts access**.

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Keep labels for screen readers | Keeps access · Hurts access |
| Hide all text | Keeps access · Hurts access |
| Offer a simple mode | Keeps access · Hurts access |
| Require fast reflexes | Keeps access · Hurts access |

## Part 3 – Dark patterns (about 5 minutes)

A **dark pattern** tricks users: a hidden cancel button, a pre-checked box, or fake timers.

**Real-life scenario:** A subscription page shows a huge “Keep” and a tiny gray “Cancel”.

**Critical thinking:** Why is this optimized for the company, not the user?

_________________________________________

**Word bank:** *Hidden cancel* · *Fake timer* · *Pre-checked box* · *Clear choice*

**Do this:** Identify the dark pattern.

| Example | Pattern |
|---|---|
| “Only 2 left!” that is not true | _____ |
| Unsubscribe link is nearly invisible | _____ |
| Option to share data is already on | _____ |

## Part 4 – Data and wellbeing (about 5 minutes)

Optimizing for time spent can harm sleep and attention. Healthy designs include breaks and limits.

**Real-life scenario:** A video app tracks only watch time and suggests more videos nonstop.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Which other metric could measure user wellbeing?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one metric that is better than just watch time.
