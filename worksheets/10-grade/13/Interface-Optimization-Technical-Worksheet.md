## Part 2 – Fitts and Hick (about 5 minutes)

**Fitts's Law**: big, close targets are faster to hit. **Hick's Law**: more choices make decisions slower.

**Real-life scenario:** A page has 15 equal buttons. Users take longer to pick. The designer groups them into 4 clear sections.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How does reducing choices speed users up?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle the faster design.

| Design | Faster? |
|---|---|
| Large Submit button near the form | Faster · Slower |
| Tiny Submit button far away | Faster · Slower |
| 4 clear menu options | Faster · Slower |
| 20 unlabeled options | Faster · Slower |

## Part 3 – Visual hierarchy (about 5 minutes)

**Hierarchy** shows what matters most using size, color, contrast, and position. The eye goes first to the biggest, boldest element.

**Real-life scenario:** In a sign-up form, the main button is large and colorful; Cancel is a plain link.

**Critical thinking:** What happens if everything is the same size and color?

_________________________________________

**Word bank:** *Size* · *Contrast* · *Position* · *Whitespace*

## Part 4 – Performance (about 5 minutes)

Speed is part of the interface. **Load time**, **lazy loading**, and **compressed images** make pages feel faster.

**Real-life scenario:** A page loads in 8 seconds. After compressing images, it loads in 2.
