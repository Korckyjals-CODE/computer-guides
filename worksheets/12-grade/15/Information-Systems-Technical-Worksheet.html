Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: An information system combines people, processes, data, software and hardware to collect, store, process and share information. Examples include student records, hospital systems and online stores.

Do this:

  1. Name an information system you used this week.
  2. Circle the components of an information system: People, process, data, software, hardware · Only computers

Part 2 – Components (about 5 minutes)

Hardware (servers, devices), software (applications), data (records), networks (connections), people (users, administrators) and processes (procedures). A system fails when any component is weak.

Real-life scenario: A school system has fast servers but staff never learned the process for entering grades.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which component is the weak point?

Part 3 – Types of systems (about 5 minutes)

Common types: TPS (transactions like sales), MIS (reports for managers), DSS (decision support), ERP (integrated business), and CRM (customer relations).

Real-life scenario: A shop uses one system for sales, another for stock and another for customers.

Critical thinking: Which type could integrate them?

Word bank: TPS · MIS · ERP

Do this: Match the system to its use.

Part 4 – Life cycle and design (about 5 minutes)

The system development life cycle includes planning, analysis, design, building, testing, deployment and maintenance. Skipping analysis often creates costly rework.

Real-life scenario: A team builds software without asking users what they need.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What problem is likely?

Do this:

  1. Write three questions to ask users during analysis.
  2. Circle the safer start: Analyze needs first · Code immediately

Part 5 – System diagram (about 5 minutes)
