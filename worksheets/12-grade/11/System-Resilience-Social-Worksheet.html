System Resilience: Social Worksheet

Grade 12 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Technical resilience fails without people, policy, and trust. Outages create stress, unequal impact, and pressure to hide problems. In this worksheet you will examine communication, equity, drills, and ethical priorities when systems are under stress.

Do this:

  1. Name a disruption that affected a community you know (school portal, city power, mobile network).
  2. Write one non-technical factor that made recovery harder or easier (communication, training, trust).

Part 2 – People as part of the resilient system (about 5 minutes)

Operators, teachers, students, and vendors are part of the system. Unclear roles, blame culture, or missing runbooks turn a small failure into chaos. Resilience includes knowing who decides, who communicates, and who can escalate.

Real-life scenario: The learning platform fails during finals week. IT knows a workaround. Teachers are not told for three hours. Students flood social media with rumors that grades were deleted.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which human/process gaps reduced resilience even if servers could be fixed quickly?


Do this: Assign each need to a role: IT, School leadership, or Teachers/students (best primary owner).

Part 3 – Equity under stress (about 5 minutes)

Disruptions hit unevenly: limited home bandwidth, shared devices, disability accommodations, or rigid timed assessments. A resilient organization plans fair alternatives, not only technical failover.

Real-life scenario: Online exam software fails. Students with mobile data finish; others cannot. Leadership insists “the window was the same for everyone,” refusing extensions.

Critical thinking: Propose one fairness action that strengthens social resilience without pretending the outage did not happen.


Do this: List two groups who may need extra support during a school-wide tech outage.

Part 4 – Drills, transparency, and ethical trade-offs (about 5 minutes)

Drills (tabletops, restore tests) build muscle memory. Transparency about known risks builds trust—hiding fragility to protect image often worsens harm. Organizations also face trade-offs: spend on resilience vs other needs; disclose vs over-alarm.

Real-life scenario: An audit finds untested backups. Leadership delays telling the community until “after the open house,” hoping nothing fails. A ransomware event hits the next week.

Do this:

  1. Draft 2–3 sentences of an honest (non-panic) community update when a critical resilience gap is found.


  2. Name one ethical tension between cost, convenience, and resilience for a school digital service.

Part 5 – Community resilience board (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Sketch a four-box board for outage readiness: Technical controls · Roles & communication · Equity supports · Drill / learn cycle.
  1. Explain why leaving any one box empty weakens the whole plan.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I see people and process as part of system resilience.
  • □ I can assign clear roles during an outage.
  • □ I can explain why outages create unequal impacts.
  • □ I can argue for drills and honest communication.
  • □ I can outline a community resilience board spanning tech and social factors.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
