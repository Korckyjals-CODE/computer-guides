## Part 2 – Person approach vs system approach (about 5 minutes)

The person approach blames individuals. The system approach asks which latent conditions (training gaps, poor UI, time pressure) invited the error—and redesigns them.

**Real-life scenario:** A wrong grade exports because columns are unlabeled similarly. Leadership suspends one student assistant; the spreadsheet UI stays identical.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What system changes address the root better than punishment alone?

## Part 3 – Reporting near-misses (about 5 minutes)

If reporting a near-miss costs you socially, people hide problems until harm is large. Psychological safety is a human-factors control.

**Real-life scenario:** A lab monitor almost bricks devices with the wrong image but stays silent, fearing blame. Next week someone else fails fully.

## Part 4 – Ethics of automation and deskilling (about 5 minutes)

Automation can reduce workload or hide complexity until failure. Human factors asks whether people still understand enough to intervene—and who is accountable.

**Real-life scenario:** An AI filter auto-flags essays; staff stop reading samples. Bias slips through unnoticed for months.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What human oversight belongs in that workflow?
