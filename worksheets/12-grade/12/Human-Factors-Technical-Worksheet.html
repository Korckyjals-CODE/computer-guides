Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Human factors studies how humans perform with tools and systems—perception, cognition, error modes, and environment—so designs prevent harm and support reliable performance.

Do this:

  1. Give an example where a confusing interface contributed to a mistake.
  2. Circle a human-factors aim: Design for predictable human performance · Assume perfect users

Part 2 – Error types and defenses (about 5 minutes)

Slips (right plan, wrong execution), mistakes (wrong plan), and lapses (memory failures) need different defenses: constraints, checklists, confirmations, training.

Real-life scenario: A student intends to unshare one file (plan OK) but clicks “Anyone with the link” for the whole folder (slip). A confirm dialog naming the scope would help.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Is that failure best fixed by “try harder” or by better constraints?

Do this: Circle the better pairing.

Part 3 – Mental models and feedback (about 5 minutes)

Users act on mental models. If the system’s feedback does not match the model, performance collapses. Visibility of system state is a human-factors essential.

Real-life scenario: A cloud sync icon looks “done” while upload still runs. The laptop closes; a draft never reaches the teacher.

Critical thinking: What state signals should the UI show?

Do this:

  1. Describe a misleading status indicator you have seen.
  2. Propose a clearer signal (text + icon behavior).

Part 4 – Workload, stress, and performance (about 5 minutes)

Under high workload or stress, tunnel vision and skill regression appear. Critical systems should simplify choices, shed nonessential alarms, and support recovery.

Real-life scenario: During a timed outage drill, five red alerts flash at once with similar wording. Operators freeze.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How would you prioritize alerts using human-factors thinking?

Do this: Build a case card: Context → Human limit/factor → Failure → Design defense. Use a school or public example.

State one measurable improvement you would expect.
