Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A design system is a shared library of design tokens (colors, spacing, type), reusable components (buttons, forms), patterns, and guidelines. It keeps products consistent and speeds up teams.

Do this:

  1. Name two products by the same company that look similar and say what they share.
  2. Circle the building blocks of a design system: Tokens and components · Random pictures

Part 2 – Tokens and components (about 5 minutes)

Design tokens store decisions as names and values (for example, color-primary = #0b5cad). Components use tokens so one change updates everywhere.

Real-life scenario: The team changes color-primary once and every button in the app updates.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could go wrong if every team made its own button style?

Part 3 – Governance and documentation (about 5 minutes)

A system needs documentation, versioning, and contribution rules. Teams must know how to use, request, and update components.

Real-life scenario: A new Date Picker is proposed, reviewed for accessibility, tested, documented, and released as v2.3.

Critical thinking: Why version a design system like software?

Word bank: Documentation · Versioning · Review · Changelog

Part 4 – Scaling and trade-offs (about 5 minutes)

A rigid system speeds work but may limit creativity. A flexible one may drift into inconsistency. Teams balance consistency, flexibility, and maintenance cost.

Real-life scenario: A team needs a special chart not in the library.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Should they build it locally or add it to the system? Justify.

Do this:

  1. Write two criteria for adding a new component to a system.
  2. Circle the stronger approach: Contribute reusable parts back · Copy and tweak in secret
