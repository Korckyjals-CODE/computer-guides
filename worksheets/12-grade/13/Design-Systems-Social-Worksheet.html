Design Systems: Social Worksheet

Grade 12 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Design systems shape the experience of millions of people. They carry values: accessibility, inclusion, brand voice, and collaboration across teams. Who decides, and who is included, matters.

Do this:

  1. Write one value a company might want its design system to express.
  2. Circle the stronger system: Includes accessibility from the start · Adds accessibility later if time allows

Part 2 – Accessibility built in (about 5 minutes)

If a component is accessible, every product using it inherits that benefit. If it is not, the barrier spreads everywhere.

Real-life scenario: A button component includes keyboard focus and sufficient contrast by default.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is it efficient to fix accessibility once, in the system?

Do this: Circle Inclusive or Exclusionary.

PracticeEffect
Contrast checked in tokensInclusive · Exclusionary

Part 3 – Collaboration and ownership (about 5 minutes)

Design systems need designers, engineers, writers, and users to work together. Clear ownership prevents neglect and conflict.

Real-life scenario: Engineers say the system slows them; designers say it is ignored. A shared roadmap helps.

Critical thinking: How can a team make contribution welcoming?

Word bank: Roadmap · Office hours · Feedback form · Credit

Do this: Choose a helpful practice.

ProblemPractice
Teams do not know the roadmap
Contributors feel ignored
People need guidance

Part 4 – Culture and inclusion (about 5 minutes)

Names, icons, examples, and imagery can include or exclude cultures and genders. Inclusive language matters.

Real-life scenario: An example form requires a gender choice with two options. Users complain.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How could the design be more respectful?

Do this:

  1. Write one change that makes a component more inclusive.
  2. Circle the inclusive choice: Flexible, optional fields · Forced narrow categories

Part 5 – Values poster (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a poster of three values your design system will protect.

Write how you would measure one of them.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know a system carries values.
  • □ I can describe accessibility by default.
  • □ I know ownership and collaboration matter.
  • □ I can think about inclusive language.
  • □ I can propose a value for a system.
