Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Data architecture is the blueprint for how data is collected, stored, integrated, and delivered. Core pieces: sources, pipelines (ETL), databases, data warehouses and lakes, and access layers.

Do this:

  1. Name one system whose data you would connect to another.
  2. Circle the process that Extracts, Transforms, and Loads data: ETL · FTP

Part 2 – Core components (about 5 minutes)

Sources produce data. ETL pipelines move and clean it. Warehouses hold structured data for analysis, while lakes hold raw data of many formats.

Real-life scenario: A store sends sales data through an ETL pipeline into a warehouse.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why clean data before loading it?

Do this: Match each component to its role. Circle one.

Part 3 – Design principles (about 5 minutes)

Good architecture is scalable, secure, reliable, and governed. Trade-offs include cost versus speed.

Real-life scenario: A company must handle ten times more data next year.

Critical thinking: Which principle matters most here?

Word bank: Scalable · Secure · Reliable · Governed

Do this: Write the principle that matches.

NeedPrinciple
Grow with demand
Protect from attacks
Keep working when parts fail

Part 4 – Integration and quality (about 5 minutes)

Combining systems needs shared definitions, master data, and quality checks so reports agree.

Real-life scenario: Sales and finance report different customer counts.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How would architecture help?

Do this:

  1. Write one step to make two systems agree on customer data.
  2. Circle the stronger fix: A single master record · Two separate lists

Part 5 – Architecture diagram (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a diagram with two sources, a pipeline, a warehouse, and a dashboard.

Write one risk and how you would reduce it.
