Smart Devices: Technical Worksheet

Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A smart device is more than a sensor or a switch. It senses, decides, connects, and acts. In this worksheet you will learn its four parts, the difference between a decision made on the device and a decision made in the cloud, what data the device creates, and what should still work when the network drops.

Do this:

  1. Name one smart device you have used or seen (speaker, watch, lock, thermostat, light, or another).
  2. In one sentence, what can it do that a simple version of the same object cannot do?

Part 2 – Four parts that work together (about 5 minutes)

Most smart devices combine four jobs. A sensor measures something, such as temperature, motion, or sound. A processor decides what that measurement means. A connection sends or receives messages, often over Wi-Fi. An action is the response: showing a message, turning a motor, changing a setting, or playing a sound. If you remove one part, the device may still turn on, but it is no longer doing the “smart” job.

Real-life scenario: A smart thermostat measures the room, compares it with the schedule, and can also take a change from a phone app. The heater or air conditioner is what actually changes the room.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which part senses, which part decides, which part needs the network, and which part changes the room?

Do this: Write Sensor, Processor, Connection, or Action.

Part 3 – On the device or in the cloud (about 5 minutes)

On the device means the processor in the house makes the decision with information it already has. In the cloud means the device sends data to a computer somewhere else and waits for an answer. A local decision can be faster and can keep working when Wi-Fi fails. A cloud decision can use a larger history, an account, or a phone that is not at home. A good design says which jobs must work with no network.

Real-life scenario: A smart lock can be opened with a keypad code stored in the lock. It can also be opened from an app when the phone is online. During a storm, the home Wi-Fi goes out. Someone arrives with the keypad code. Someone else tries the app from the driveway.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which method should still work, and why is that a safety choice rather than only a convenience?

Do this: Mark where each job should be handled. Write On the device or In the cloud.

Job Where it should work Why
Unlock with the keypad code during an outage
Show a month-long temperature graph in the app
Turn the heater on when the room is below the set point
Let a family member who is across town change the thermostat

Part 4 – The record the device keeps (about 5 minutes)

A smart device does not only act. It often creates data: temperature over time, when a lock opened, which account sent a command, or a short voice clip after a wake word. Data can help the device learn a schedule. It can also describe people’s routines more closely than a normal appliance would.

Critical thinking: A fitness watch can store heart rate on the watch or upload every reading to an account. Name one benefit of uploading and one risk. Then say which choice you would want as the default, and why.

Do this: For each device, list one kind of data it might store and whether that data must leave the house for the main job to work. Write Must leave or Can stay.

Part 5 – Trace one device (about 5 minutes)

A clear diagram shows what stays in the home and what crosses the network. That diagram is a way to check the design before anyone buys the device.

Do this:

  1. Choose one device: smart speaker, smart lock, or smart thermostat. Sketch sensor → processor → action, and show the connection. Label one message that stays in the home and one message that leaves.
  1. Write one sentence. What should this device still do if the connection fails?

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can tell a decision on the device from a decision in the cloud.

  • □ I can identify data a device creates and whether that data must leave the home.
