## Part 2 – Attention and working memory (about 5 minutes)

People hold only a few chunks in **working memory**. Long codes, multi-field forms without save, and hidden instructions overload users.

**Real-life scenario:** A password reset shows a 16-character code for 10 seconds with no copy button. Users mis-type it repeatedly.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How would you redesign that step to respect memory limits?

## Part 3 – Motor precision and Fitts-like ideas (about 5 minutes)

Small targets far from the cursor are slower and more error-prone. Touch targets need size and spacing; keyboard focus order matters too.

**Real-life scenario:** A “Delete account” button sits tiny next to “Save.” Tired users hit Delete.

## Part 4 – Fatigue, speed, and error rates (about 5 minutes)

As fatigue rises, speed may stay high while **error rate** climbs. Good systems allow breaks, autosave, and undo rather than punishing humans for being human.

**Real-life scenario:** During a 90-minute data entry drill with no breaks, late entries show swapped digits.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Which system feature would best protect data quality here?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Feature | Helps under fatigue? |
|---|---|
| Autosave every minute | Yes · No |
| No undo allowed ever | Yes · No |
| Progress saved if the tab closes | Yes · No |
