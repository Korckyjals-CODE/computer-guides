## Part 2 – Types of system issues (about 5 minutes)

Classifying helps you choose the first tests: **hardware** (physical parts), **software** (apps/OS), **network** (connectivity and online services), or **configuration** (settings, accounts, permissions). Wrong categories waste time.

**Real-life scenario:** Nobody in the lab can open the online classroom, but local files and offline videos still work. Monitors and projectors are fine.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why does this case point more to network or an online service than to a broken screen?

## Part 3 – Symptoms, isolation, and evidence (about 5 minutes)

Strong diagnosis records: **what fails**, **since when**, **how many devices or accounts**, and **what changed recently**. Change one variable at a time. Capture error text—guessing without evidence slows everyone down.

**Real-life scenario:** Only computer 7 cannot print. Computers 6 and 8 work. Yesterday someone moved desk 7 and the cable looked loose afterward.

**Critical thinking:** What first test would you run, and what evidence would you look for?

## Part 4 – Restart, document, and escalate (about 5 minutes)

**Restarting** may clear temporary states, but it is not analysis if the issue returns. **Documenting** (symptom, tests, result) helps support staff. **Escalating** means requesting help when risk or complexity is beyond safe self-repair.

**Real-life scenario:** A student restarts three times a machine that smells like burning and keeps forcing power on. The odor signals a possible electrical fault that needs an adult or IT—not more retries.
