System Issues: Social Worksheet

Grade 8 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: System issues affect people and groups—they create stress, rumors, and unfair blame. A mature social response communicates clearly, avoids blaming without evidence, and looks for fair fixes. In this worksheet you will examine impact, shared responsibility, and ethical communication during outages.

Do this:

  1. Name a failure that affected more than one person at school or home.
  2. Write one emotion that often appears in those moments.

Part 2 – Uneven impact on people (about 5 minutes)

Not everyone is hurt equally by an outage. People with one device, limited home connectivity, or a tight deadline lose more. Pretending “everyone had the same chance” can be unfair when access differs.

Real-life scenario: The assignment portal drops one hour before the deadline. Students with mobile data finish; students who only have school Wi-Fi cannot submit.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What adjustment would be fairer than insisting everyone had equal opportunity?


Do this: List two profiles that might be hit harder by a school system outage.

Part 3 – Blame without evidence vs learning (about 5 minutes)

Pointing at a person without data discourages early reporting. A learning culture asks what failed in the process (permissions, backups, training, time) and how to prevent a repeat.

Real-life scenario: A shared folder disappears. Before checking trash, versions, or permissions, someone shouts, “Who did this?” Nobody admits moving files—they fear blame.

Critical thinking: Write one learning-first question (not a blame question) you would ask first.

Do this: Label each reaction Blame first or Learning first.

ReactionType
“You’re a disaster—never touch the server again.”
“Let’s check permissions and whether a backup exists.”
“Let’s note the step that failed and one improvement.”

Part 4 – Communication during an outage (about 5 minutes)

A strong message states what is broken, who is affected, what is known, and when the next update will come—even if the root cause is still unclear. Silence feeds rumors.

Real-life scenario: The grades system fails. For three hours there is no announcement. A rumor spreads that “all grades were deleted,” though only login was broken.

Do this: Draft a short notice (2–3 sentences) that is honest and does not blame anyone.



Part 5 – Group response board (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a response board with four boxes: Facts · Impact on people · What we will change · How we communicate.
  1. Explain why human impact belongs next to technical facts.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can explain how a system issue can hit people unevenly.
  • □ I can tell blame reactions from learning reactions.
  • □ I can draft a clear notice during an outage.
  • □ I value honest reporting without inventing rumors.
  • □ I can propose a response board that includes people, not only tech.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
