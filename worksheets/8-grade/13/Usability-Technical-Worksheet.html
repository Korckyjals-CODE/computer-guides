Usability: Technical Worksheet

Grade 8 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Usability measures how easy and pleasant it is to use a product. Common usability goals are being learnable, efficient, memorable, error-tolerant, and satisfying.

Do this:

  1. Name one app or website that is easy to use, and one that is hard.
  2. Circle the usability goal that means “easy to learn”: Learnability · Color

Part 2 – Usability goals (about 5 minutes)

Five goals guide usability: learnability (easy to learn), efficiency (fast to use), memorability (easy to remember), errors (few and easy to fix), and satisfaction (pleasant).

Real-life scenario: Kai opens a new app and completes the task in one minute without help.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which goals does Kai's experience show?

Do this: Match each situation with a goal.

SituationGoal
A new user finishes without helpLearnability · Efficiency · Errors
A frequent user finishes in few stepsLearnability · Efficiency · Errors
A mistake is fixed with UndoLearnability · Efficiency · Errors

Part 3 – Testing usability (about 5 minutes)

A usability test has a user try tasks while observers note problems. We measure time, success, and mistakes.

Real-life scenario: Five students test a school website. Three cannot find the schedule.

Critical thinking: What would you change after seeing this result?

Word bank: Task · Observer · Success rate · Time

Do this: Complete each description.

DescriptionTerm
A job given to the tester
Person who watches and notes
Percentage who finish the task

Part 4 – Improving a design (about 5 minutes)

After testing, designers fix the biggest problems first. Small changes, like clearer labels, can help a lot.

Real-life scenario: The schedule link is renamed from “Resources” to “Class Schedule.” More students find it.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How do you decide which problem to fix first?

Do this:

  1. Write one usability problem you have seen and a fix.
  2. Circle the better label: Class Schedule · Resources & Misc.

Part 5 – Usability sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a before/after of a screen where you improved one label or button.

Write the usability goal your change supports.
