## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** Usability is about real people. Products that are hard to use can exclude children, older adults, people with disabilities, or people who speak other languages. **Inclusive usability** gives more people equal chances.

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one person who might find a product hard to use.

   _________________________

2. **Circle** the idea that is true: Usable products include more people · Usability matters only for experts

## Part 2 – Who is left out? (about 5 minutes)

When designers test only with people like themselves, they may miss problems others face.

**Real-life scenario:** An app has only red and green status icons. A color-blind user cannot tell them apart.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Who else could this design leave out?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Inclusive** or **Excludes**.

| Design | Result |
|---|---|
| Icons with text labels | Inclusive · Excludes |
| Only color to show errors | Inclusive · Excludes |
| Adjustable text size | Inclusive · Excludes |
| Audio-only instructions | Inclusive · Excludes |

## Part 3 – Testing with different users (about 5 minutes)

Testing with a variety of users, including different ages and abilities, finds more problems.

**Real-life scenario:** A library tests its website with teens, older adults, and a blind user.

## Part 4 – Respectful feedback (about 5 minutes)

Users should feel safe giving honest feedback. Designers should listen and thank them.

**Real-life scenario:** A student tells the teacher a form is confusing. The teacher thanks her and fixes it.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How can you give feedback kindly?
