## How to use this worksheet

- Do the parts **in order** from top to bottom.
- Each part has a **time** so you know how long to spend.
- Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

---

## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** A **name** tells us what something is. Every picture or story on a computer has a **name**, so we can find it again.

**Do this:**

1. **Write** the name of a pet, a toy, or a friend.

## Part 2 – Names for things (about 5 minutes)

Things have names. A dog is a **dog**. A chair is a **chair**. A name helps people know which thing we mean.

**Real-life scenario:** Ben says, “Please bring me the thing.” His mom does not know which thing he wants.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why does a clear name help?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Is it a name? | Answer |
|---|---|
| Apple | Yes · No |
| Blue chair | Yes · No |
| Hmm | Yes · No |
| Dog | Yes · No |

## Part 3 – Names on the computer (about 5 minutes)

Files on a computer have names too. A drawing of a house can be called **House**. The name shows what is inside.

**Real-life scenario:** Lia saves a drawing as “aaa”. Later she cannot tell which drawing it is.

**Critical thinking:** What name would be better for her drawing?

_________________________________________

## Part 4 – Short and clear (about 5 minutes)

A good name is **short** and says what the file is. “My Cat” is clearer than “Thing 1”.

**Real-life scenario:** Sam has three files: Cat, Cat2, Cat3. He cannot tell them apart.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How can Sam change the names?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

1. **Write** a name for a drawing of your family.

   _________________________
