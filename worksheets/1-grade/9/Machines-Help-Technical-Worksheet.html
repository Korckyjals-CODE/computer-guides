Machines Help: Technical Worksheet

Grade 1 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A machine is a tool that helps a person do a job. You can name a part you see, like a button or a handle. In this worksheet you will name a machine, name one part, and say what job it helps with.

Do this:

  1. Have you seen a machine help a person? Circle: Yes or No
  2. Name one machine you know.

Part 2 – Parts you can see (about 5 minutes)

A machine has parts. A button or a handle can start the job. Wheels, a blade, or a door can do some of the work. The machine does not pick the job. A person does.

Real-life scenario: You put a pencil in the class sharpener. You turn the handle or press the button. The sharpener twists the pencil. You do not have to scrape it with a little knife.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What do you do? What does the machine do?

Do this: Use the word bank. Write one part and one job.

Word bank: Handle · Button · Door · Sharpens a pencil · Washes clothes · Opens for people

Part 3 – Start and stop (about 5 minutes)

You start a machine when you press, turn, or walk up to it. A machine should stop when the job is done, or when a person turns it off. A stop keeps the job from going on and on.

Real-life scenario: You push the toaster lever down. The bread gets warm. Then the toast pops up. If the toast never popped up, the bread could burn.

Critical thinking: Why does a machine need a way to stop? Write one sentence.

Do this: Write Start or Stop.

What happens Start or Stop?
You press the button on a fan
The fan blades slow down and the fan is off
You walk near the school door
The door closes after people go through

Part 4 – The same job again (about 5 minutes)

Some machines do the same job again and again. They do not get tired. A person still watches to keep the job safe.

Real-life scenario: A dishwasher washes many plates. Someone still has to put the plates in and close the door.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Name one job a machine can do again and again. Name one job you still want a person to do.

Do this:

  1. A machine folds 8 shirts in 4 minutes. A person folds 8 shirts in 12 minutes. Circle who is faster at this job: The machine or The person
  2. The machine stops in the middle. Circle who should check it: The machine alone or A person

Part 5 – Draw a helper (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a machine that could help in your classroom. Label the part you use to start it. Label the job it does.
  1. Write one sentence. Who does your machine help?

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can name a machine and the job it helps with.
  • □ I can name one part I can see, like a button or a handle.
  • □ I can tell a start from a stop.
  • □ I know a machine can do a job again, and a person still checks it.
  • □ I can draw a classroom machine and label its part and its job.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
