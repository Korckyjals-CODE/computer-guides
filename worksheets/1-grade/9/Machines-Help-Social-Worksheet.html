Machines Help: Social Worksheet

Grade 1 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A machine can help people. People still share, take turns, and stay safe. In this worksheet you will think about who a machine helps, how to be careful, and how to be fair when many children need the same machine.

Do this:

  1. Name one machine that helps you at home or at school.
  2. Circle who it helps: Me · My family or class · Both

Part 2 – Who gets the help (about 5 minutes)

A machine can help one person and still leave a job for someone else. Help is not the same for every person.

Real-life scenario: A drinking fountain turns on when hands are near it. A small child cannot reach the sensor. A taller child gets a drink right away. The small child still needs help.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Who can use the fountain alone? Who still needs a person?

Do this: Fill in the table.

Machine Who it helps One job a person still does
Drinking fountain
Fan in the classroom
Book drop at the library

Part 3 – Stay safe (about 5 minutes)

Some machines spin, get hot, or move by themselves. A safe choice means you wait, you keep hands away, or you ask a grown-up.

Real-life scenario: The class fan is on. It helps the room feel cool. A friend wants to touch the spinning part “just to see.”

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should you say? What should the friend do?

Do this: Circle the safer choice.

What you see Safer choice (circle one)
A fan is spinning Touch the blades · Keep hands away
Toast pops up and the toaster is hot Grab it right away · Wait, or ask a grown-up
A door starts to close on a backpack Jump through · Wait until the way is clear

Part 4 – Take turns (about 5 minutes)

When many people need one machine, the class needs a fair rule. A fair rule is easy to say. One person does not keep the machine the whole time.

Real-life scenario: One pencil sharpener helps the whole class write. Three children need it at once. One child sharpens every pencil in a full box. The others wait. Their work cannot start.

Critical thinking: Write one fair rule. Say who goes next, or how many pencils one person may sharpen.

Do this: Number these actions 1, 2, and 3. 1 happens first.

Action Order (1, 2, or 3)
The next child in line uses the sharpener
Children who need a sharp pencil line up
The first child steps aside after one or two pencils

Part 5 – A sign that helps (about 5 minutes)

A short sign can remind people to wait, take turns, or keep hands safe.

Do this:

  1. Draw a sign for one machine at school. Write the words of your rule on the sign.
  1. Write one sentence. Who is your sign trying to help?

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can say who a machine helps and who still needs a person.
  • □ I can choose a safe way to be near a machine that spins, moves, or gets hot.
  • □ I can write a fair rule for a machine the class shares.
  • □ I can put sharing steps in a kind order.
  • □ I can make a sign that tells people how to use a machine.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
