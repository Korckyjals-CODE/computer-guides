Keyboard Basics: Technical Worksheet

Grade 1 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A keyboard is the set of buttons (called keys) you press to put letters, numbers, and spaces into a computer or tablet. In this worksheet you will find letter keys, the space bar, and the Enter key, and practice careful pressing.

Do this:

  1. Name one place you have seen a keyboard (school, home, library…).
  2. Circle what a keyboard helps you do: Type words · Draw with a crayon · Eat lunch

Part 2 – What is a keyboard? (about 5 minutes)

A keyboard sits in front of you (or appears on a tablet screen). Each key is one button. When you press a key softly, the computer shows a letter, number, or does a small job.

Real-life scenario: Lina sits at the school computer. She sees rows of keys with letters. She presses A. The letter A appears on the screen.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why do keyboards have many keys instead of just one big button?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 3 – Letter keys and the space bar (about 5 minutes)

Letter keys type the alphabet. The long space bar at the bottom makes a blank space between words so they do not stick together.

Real-life scenario: Omar types his name as “OmarLopez” with no space. His teacher cannot read the two words. He presses the space bar between Omar and Lopez.

Critical thinking: What happens to words if you forget the space bar?

Do this: Circle the matching job.

Part 4 – Enter and careful fingers (about 5 minutes)

The Enter key (sometimes called Return) starts a new line, like moving down on a page. Press keys with soft fingers—hard slamming can hurt the keys.

Real-life scenario: Mia finishes one short sentence. She presses Enter. Her next words start on a new line under the first sentence.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: When would you want a new line on the screen?

Do this:

  1. Circle the safer press: Soft finger · Hard slam
  2. Write one word you would type with letter keys.

Part 5 – Draw a keyboard tip (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Think of one tip for using a keyboard (soft press, use space bar, or find Enter).

Do this: Draw a simple keyboard. Mark the space bar or the Enter key.

Write your tip in a few words under your drawing.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know a keyboard has many keys.
  • □ I know letter keys type the alphabet.
  • □ I know the space bar makes a blank between words.
  • □ I know Enter can start a new line.
  • □ I know to press keys softly.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
