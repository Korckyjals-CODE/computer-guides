## Part 2 – What is a keyboard? (about 5 minutes)

A keyboard sits in front of you (or appears on a tablet screen). Each **key** is one button. When you press a key softly, the computer shows a letter, number, or does a small job.

**Real-life scenario:** Lina sits at the school computer. She sees rows of keys with letters. She presses **A**. The letter A appears on the screen.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why do keyboards have many keys instead of just one big button?

## Part 3 – Letter keys and the space bar (about 5 minutes)

**Letter keys** type the alphabet. The long **space bar** at the bottom makes a blank space between words so they do not stick together.

**Real-life scenario:** Omar types his name as “OmarLopez” with no space. His teacher cannot read the two words. He presses the space bar between Omar and Lopez.

**Critical thinking:** What happens to words if you forget the space bar?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle the matching job.

| Key | Job |
|---|---|
| A, B, C … | Letter · Space · Enter |
| Long bar at the bottom | Letter · Space · Enter |

## Part 4 – Enter and careful fingers (about 5 minutes)

The **Enter** key (sometimes called Return) starts a **new line**, like moving down on a page. Press keys with soft fingers—hard slamming can hurt the keys.

**Real-life scenario:** Mia finishes one short sentence. She presses Enter. Her next words start on a new line under the first sentence.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** When would you want a new line on the screen?
