Lab Rules: Technical Worksheet

Grade 4 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Lab rules are clear steps for entering, using, and leaving a computer lab. They protect people and equipment. You will sort rules by type and put procedures in order.

Do this:

  1. Name one place at school where computers are shared (lab, cart, library).
  2. Write one lab rule you already know.

Part 2 – Why lab rules exist (about 5 minutes)

Lab rules are not random. They reduce spills, broken cables, lost work, and wasted time. A good rule tells you what to do and often when to do it.

Real-life scenario: Class A rushes in, bags swing over keyboards, and drinks sit on desks. Class B enters quietly, bags stay in a marked zone, and drinks stay outside. Class B starts work five minutes sooner.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which class followed stronger lab rules? How can you tell?

Do this: Label each rule People, Equipment, or Login/Logout.

Lab ruleType
Walk—do not run—to your assigned seat
Do not unplug cables without permission
Log out and shut down as instructed
Keep food and drinks outside the lab

Part 3 – Enter and exit procedures (about 5 minutes)

A procedure is a set of steps in order. Entering: bag zone → seat → wait for the start signal. Exiting: save → log out → push in chair → leave equipment ready.

Real-life scenario: The bell rings. Kai jumps up and leaves without logging out. The next class can see Kai’s open files and cannot start their own accounts.

Critical thinking: Which exit step did Kai skip, and why does that matter?

Do this: Number these exit steps 1, 2, 3, and 4.

StepOrder (1–4)
Push in your chair and leave the desk clear
Save your work
Log out (or shut down) as the teacher says
Check that headphones and mice are back in place

Part 4 – Equipment handling rules (about 5 minutes)

Handle keyboards, mice, headphones, and screens gently. Use only the software and websites your teacher allows. Report damaged equipment instead of hiding it.

Real-life scenario: A headphone cord is tangled under a chair. Instead of asking for help, a student yanks hard. The plug snaps off inside the jack.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What safer action could replace yanking?

Do this: Write Follows rules or Breaks rules.

ActionFollows or Breaks?
Ask before connecting a personal USB drive
Change cable setups without permission
Tell the teacher a key is stuck
Open a game site during a research task

Part 5 – Map the lab flow (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Sketch a simple lab (door, bag zone, seats, teacher desk). Draw arrows for enter and exit flow. Label three rules.
  1. Write one sentence about the rule that helps most at the start of class.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can explain why lab rules protect people and equipment.
  • □ I can sort rules as people, equipment, or login/logout.
  • □ I can put exit steps in a sensible order.
  • □ I know gentle ways to handle lab equipment.
  • □ I can map enter/exit flow and label key rules.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
