Lab Rules: Social Worksheet

Grade 4 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Lab rules work best when the class shares responsibility. Fairness, helping, and honest reporting keep the lab ready for everyone. You will practice community habits around the rules.

Do this:

  1. Name who uses the computer lab besides your class.
  2. Circle who should follow lab rules: Only new students · Only the teacher · Everyone in the lab

Part 2 – Fair turns and shared seats (about 5 minutes)

Stations and headphones are shared tools. Taking more than your turn, swapping seats without asking, or hogging a working mouse hurts classmates.

Real-life scenario: Only one mouse works well at a table. One student keeps it the whole period. Two partners cannot finish their research slides.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Write one fair sharing rule for that mouse.

Do this: Mark Fair or Unfair.

ActionFair or Unfair?
Rotate the working mouse every 10 minutes
Hide headphones so only you can use them
Ask before switching to an empty station
Take over a classmate’s computer without asking

Part 3 – Help without taking over (about 5 minutes)

Helping is part of community. Show a partner where a menu is. Do not grab the mouse and finish their work for them. Ask, “Want a hint?” before you jump in.

Real-life scenario: Priya cannot find the save button. A classmate slides over, takes the mouse, saves the file, and closes the window before Priya can see how.

Critical thinking: How could the helper teach Priya instead of taking over?

Do this: Circle the better helper choice.

SituationBetter choice

Part 4 – When rules are broken (about 5 minutes)

Mistakes happen. Own them, fix what you can, and tell an adult when needed. Covering up a spill or a crack can put the next class at risk.

Real-life scenario: Omar bumps a bottle near a keyboard. A little water reaches the desk edge. He wipes with his sleeve and hopes no one notices.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should Omar do next to protect people and equipment?

Do this: Number these responsibility steps 1, 2, and 3.

StepOrder (1, 2, or 3)
Tell a teacher what happened
Stop using the wet area and keep others away
Notice the spill or damage

Part 5 – Lab community poster (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Design a poster with three community lab habits (fair turns, helpful hints, honest reporting).
  1. Write who the poster helps besides your own class.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I see lab rules as a shared responsibility.
  • □ I can name fair ways to share seats, mice, and headphones.
  • □ I know how to help without taking over.
  • □ I know to report spills or damage honestly.
  • □ I can design a short lab community poster.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
