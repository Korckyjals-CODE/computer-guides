Navigation: Technical Worksheet

Grade 4 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Navigation means moving around a computer or website to find what you need. You use menus, folders, links, the back button, and tabs to get where you want to go.

Do this:

  1. Name one place on a computer you often navigate to (for example, a folder or a website).
  2. Circle the tool that takes you to the previous page: Back button · Print button

Part 2 – Menus, links, and buttons (about 5 minutes)

A menu lists choices. A link takes you to another page. A button performs an action. Knowing the difference helps you navigate faster.

Real-life scenario: Eva wants her science notes. She clicks Documents, then the Science folder.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is it useful to know what each element does?

Part 3 – Paths and folders (about 5 minutes)

A path shows where a file lives, like Documents > School > Science. Folders inside folders form a tree.

Real-life scenario: Leo says: “My file is in Documents > School > Math > Homework.”

Critical thinking: How does a path help someone else find your file?

Word bank: Folder · Path · Back · Address bar

Do this: Write the term that matches.

DescriptionTerm
Shows the route to a file
A container for files
Returns to the previous screen

Part 4 – Getting unlost (about 5 minutes)

If you get lost, use Back, Home, or search. Browser tabs let you keep several pages open, but too many tabs cause confusion.

Real-life scenario: Mia has 14 tabs open and cannot find her homework page.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could Mia do to feel less lost?

Do this:

  1. List two ways to find your way back.
  2. Circle the habit that helps navigation: Name files clearly · Leave everything on the desktop

Part 5 – Navigation map (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a simple map of folders: Documents with two subfolders and a file inside.

Write the path to your favorite school file.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can tell menus, links, and buttons apart.
  • □ I know what a path is.
  • □ I can use Back, Home, and search.
  • □ I know too many tabs can confuse me.
  • □ I can read a folder path.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
