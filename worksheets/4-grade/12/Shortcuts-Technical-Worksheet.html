Shortcuts: Technical Worksheet

Grade 4 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A keyboard shortcut is a key combination that does a job quickly (for example Ctrl+C to copy). You will name common shortcuts, match them to actions, and practice safe use.

Do this:

  1. Name one action you do often on a computer (copy, paste, undo, save…).
  2. Circle what a shortcut uses: Key combinations · Only the mouse forever · Food wrappers

Part 2 – Modifier keys (about 5 minutes)

Modifier keys (Ctrl, Alt, Shift, and on some computers Cmd) are held while you press another key. Alone they usually do little; together they trigger a command.

Real-life scenario: Maya holds Ctrl and taps C. Her selected sentence is copied. She did not open a menu with the mouse.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why might a shortcut be faster than hunting through menus?

Part 3 – Common school shortcuts (about 5 minutes)

Useful starters: Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+V paste, Ctrl+X cut, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+S save. (On Mac, Cmd often replaces Ctrl.)

Real-life scenario: Luis deletes a paragraph by mistake. He presses Ctrl+Z and the paragraph returns.

Critical thinking: When would Undo be safer than retyping everything?

Part 4 – Select, then shortcut (about 5 minutes)

Many shortcuts need a selection first. Click-drag or Shift+arrow to highlight text, then copy or cut. Shortcuts on empty selection may do nothing useful.

Real-life scenario: Noor presses Ctrl+C with nothing highlighted. Nothing useful copies. She selects a sentence, then Ctrl+C works.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should you check before pressing Copy?

Do this:

  1. Write the two-step order: (1) ________ (2) press the shortcut.
  2. Circle the better habit: Select text first · Mash Ctrl+C hoping something happens

Part 5 – Shortcut cheat card (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a small cheat card with at least three shortcuts and what they do.

Star the shortcut you want to practice most and write why.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know a shortcut is a key combination for a quick job.
  • □ I know modifier keys work with other keys.
  • □ I can match common shortcuts (copy, paste, undo, save).
  • □ I select text before many edit shortcuts.
  • □ I can make a small cheat card.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
