Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A sensor detects a change in the world and sends a signal to the rest of a system. In this worksheet you will learn common sensor types, what a threshold is, how a controller and an actuator use the signal, and why a sensor can be wrong.

Part 2 – What a sensor measures (about 5 minutes)

A sensor does not “understand” a place. It measures one kind of change. A light sensor measures brightness. A temperature sensor measures how hot or cold something is. A motion sensor notices movement. A distance sensor measures how far away an object is. The measurement becomes a signal the system can use.

Real-life scenario: A streetlight stays off at noon and turns on at dusk. Nobody flips a switch at the pole.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which sensor is doing the work, and what change is it measuring? What would go wrong if that sensor were covered with paint?

Do this: Match each place to the sensor that fits. Use each sensor once.

Part 3 – The line that triggers an action (about 5 minutes)

A threshold is the line the system waits for. Below that line, or above it, the system acts. A streetlight might turn on when brightness falls below a set level. A thermostat might start the heat when the room falls below the temperature people chose. If the threshold is too sensitive, the system reacts to tiny changes. If it is not sensitive enough, it reacts too late.

Critical thinking: A hallway light turns on when anyone walks by, then turns off after 10 seconds of no motion. A student stands still to read a poster. What happens, and how would you change the threshold or the timer?

Do this: For each row, name the sensor and write a sensible threshold in your own words.

Situation Sensor Threshold (when it should act)
Classroom gets too warm in the afternoon
Porch light should come on after sunset
Automatic faucet should run only when hands are under it

Part 4 – From sensing to action (about 5 minutes)

A sensor alone does not open a door or turn on a fan. A controller reads the signal and decides. An actuator does the physical action: a motor, a lamp, a lock, or a valve. The loop is sensor → controller → actuator. Then the world changes, and the sensor can measure again.

Real-life scenario: A robot vacuum senses a chair leg, decides to turn, and its wheels change direction. If it only sensed the leg and never turned, it would keep bumping.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which part measures, which part decides, and which part moves?

Part 5 – When the reading is wrong (about 5 minutes)

A sensor reports what it can detect from where it sits. Dust, glare, a bad aim, or a sensor in the wrong spot creates a false reading. The controller may then make a reasonable decision from bad information.

Do this: Fill in a likely false reading and one fix that does not require replacing the whole system.

Problem Likely false reading One fix
A light sensor on a streetlight is covered in dust
A temperature sensor sits in direct sun on a windowsill
A motion sensor faces a swaying curtain, not the doorway
