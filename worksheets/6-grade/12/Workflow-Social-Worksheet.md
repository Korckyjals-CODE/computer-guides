## Part 2 – Handoffs (about 5 minutes)

A **handoff** is when work moves from one person to another. Good handoffs include status, file link, and what is still needed.

**Real-life scenario:** Alex finishes research but only says “done” in chat with no link. Blair spends a period hunting for notes.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What three details belong in a good handoff message?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Handoff content | Include? |
|---|---|
| Link to the file | Yes · No |
| What is finished vs missing | Yes · No |
| Vague “k” with no context | Yes · No |

## Part 3 – Fair timelines (about 5 minutes)

Workflows fail socially when one person is always waiting on another with no agreed deadline. Negotiate times that fit real constraints.

**Real-life scenario:** Maya cannot edit until 9 p.m. because of chores. The team “deadline” was 6 p.m. without asking her.

**Critical thinking:** How can a team set deadlines that are efficient and fair?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

   _________________________

2. **Circle** fair practice: Check each member’s constraints · Assume everyone is free after school

## Part 4 – Blame vs process fixes (about 5 minutes)

When a step fails, improve the **process** (checklist, reminder, template) instead of only blaming a person.

**Real-life scenario:** A citation step was skipped. Leadership only scolds one student; nobody adds a checklist to the workflow.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What process fix would prevent a repeat?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Response | Process-focused? |
|---|---|
| Add a “citations done?” checkbox before submit | Yes · No |
| Call someone lazy and move on | Yes · No |
| Review the workflow together for gaps | Yes · No |
