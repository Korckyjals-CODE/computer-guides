## Part 2 – Inputs, process, output (about 5 minutes)

Every workflow has **inputs** (files, notes, data), a **process** (edit, format, calculate), and an **output** (final file, print, upload). Naming each part clarifies the plan.

**Real-life scenario:** For a science report: inputs = lab notes + images; process = write and insert figures; output = PDF in the class folder.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What goes wrong if you start formatting before you have your facts?

## Part 3 – Checkpoints and saves (about 5 minutes)

Build **checkpoints**: after research, after draft, after peer check. Save with versions (v1, v2) or clear dates so you can roll back.

**Real-life scenario:** Elena overwrites her only file with a bad edit and has no earlier copy. A simple “Essay-v1” backup would have helped.

## Part 4 – Bottlenecks (about 5 minutes)

A **bottleneck** is a step that delays everything (waiting for a login, missing image, unclear instructions). Spotting bottlenecks lets you reorder or prepare earlier.

**Real-life scenario:** The class cannot finish posters because the shared image folder was never uploaded. Design waits on media.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How could the workflow move image gathering earlier?
