Efficiency: Technical Worksheet

Grade 6 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Efficiency means doing a task using the fewest steps, little time, and good quality. With computers, efficient work uses shortcuts, templates, organized files, and a plan.

Do this:

  1. Name one task on a computer that takes you a long time.
  2. Circle the efficient approach: Plan first, then work · Start randomly

Part 2 – Measuring efficiency (about 5 minutes)

We can compare methods by counting steps and time. Fewer steps with the same result is usually more efficient.

Real-life scenario: To rename five files, Ana does it one by one in 10 steps. Ben uses a pattern and finishes in 4 steps.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Which method is more efficient? What if Ben's pattern makes mistakes?

Part 3 – Shortcuts and templates (about 5 minutes)

Shortcuts (like Ctrl+S to save) and templates (a ready-made layout) save repeated steps.

Real-life scenario: Rita uses a report template with headings already placed.

Critical thinking: When is a template not helpful?

Word bank: Ctrl+S · Ctrl+Z · Template · Ctrl+F

Do this: Match each need with a tool.

NeedTool
Save quickly
Undo a mistake
Start a document with a layout

Part 4 – Workflow planning (about 5 minutes)

A workflow is the order of steps in a task. A good workflow avoids repeated work and mistakes.

Real-life scenario: Marco collects pictures, writes text, and then designs his poster in that order.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could go wrong if he designed first?

Do this:

  1. Write a 3-step workflow for a homework presentation.
  2. Circle the best habit: Save early and often · Save only at the end

Part 5 – Workflow sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a flow of 4 boxes with arrows for a task you do on a computer.

Write one change that would make it faster.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know efficiency means fewer steps with quality.
  • □ I can use shortcuts.
  • □ I know a template helps.
  • □ I can plan a simple workflow.
  • □ I can compare two methods.
