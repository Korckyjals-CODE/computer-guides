How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.

  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A naming convention is a consistent pattern for names. It tells you where to put the subject, the date, the number, and the separator, so every file follows the same style.

Do this:

  1. Name a pattern you notice in school, such as a schedule or a uniform.
  2. Circle a convention is: A consistent pattern · A random choice

Part 2 – Common conventions (about 5 minutes)

Conventions include camelCase (myFile), snake_case (my_file), kebab-case (my-file), and Title Case (My File). Each is easy to read when used consistently.

Real-life scenario: A team mixes myFile, my_file and My-File in one folder.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is mixing styles a problem?

Do this: Circle the style: camel, snake or kebab.

Part 3 – Order matters (about 5 minutes)

Conventions say what comes first. Putting the date first keeps files in time order. Putting the subject first keeps topics together.

Real-life scenario: A teacher wants all science files together and all math files together.

Critical thinking: Which part should come first?

Word bank: subject · date · version

Part 4 – Applying a convention (about 5 minutes)

A convention is useful only when everyone applies it the same way. A checklist helps.

Real-life scenario: A student writes Science_plants-2026 11 30 V1.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What is inconsistent?

Do this:

  1. Rewrite the name in snake_case: Science plants 2026 11 30.
  2. Circle the consistent name: science_plants_2026-11-30_v1 · Science plants-2026 11 30 V1
