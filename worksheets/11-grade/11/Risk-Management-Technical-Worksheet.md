## Part 2 – Identify risks vs vague fears (about 5 minutes)

A useful risk statement names an **asset** (what you care about), a **threat** or hazard, and a possible **consequence**. “Something bad with the network” is too vague to manage. Clear identification enables assessment and controls.

**Real-life scenario:** A robotics club stores competition code only on one laptop. Vague fear: “We might lose stuff.” Clear risk: “If the laptop disk fails before finals, we lose the only build and cannot compete.”

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why does rewriting vague fears into asset–threat–consequence statements help teams act?

## Part 3 – Likelihood vs impact (about 5 minutes)

**Likelihood** is how probable an event is. **Impact** is how severe the harm would be if it occurs. High impact + low likelihood (rare catastrophic disk failure) and high likelihood + lower impact (frequent brief Wi-Fi blips) need different responses. Rough scales (Low / Medium / High) are enough for classroom analysis.

**Real-life scenario:** Risk A: ransomware encrypts the student information system (low–medium likelihood for a well-patched school, very high impact). Risk B: a club printer jams weekly (high likelihood, low impact if a backup printer exists).

**Critical thinking:** Which risk deserves priority spending if the budget allows only one major control—and why? Use likelihood and impact in your answer.

## Part 4 – Treat risks: mitigate, transfer, avoid, accept (about 5 minutes)

After assessment, choose a treatment: **Mitigate** (reduce likelihood or impact with controls), **Transfer** (shift some consequence, e.g. insurance or a vendor SLA), **Avoid** (do not do the activity), or **Accept** (knowingly live with residual risk when cost of more control outweighs benefit). Acceptance should be deliberate—not accidental neglect.

**Real-life scenario:** A journalism site collects tips. Mitigate: MFA and encryption. Avoid: refuse anonymous uploads entirely (hurts mission). Accept: residual risk that a determined attacker still breaches despite controls—documented and reviewed yearly.

**Do this:** Choose the **best-fit treatment** for each case (Mitigate / Transfer / Avoid / Accept).
