Ergonomics: Social Worksheet

Grade 11 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Ergonomics is social when labs, jobs, and homes share equipment. Fair access to adjustable chairs, breaks, and accommodations prevents harm—especially for people already in pain.

Do this:

  1. Describe a shared computer space (lab, library, café).
  2. Circle a social ergonomics issue: One-size desks for all bodies · Adjustable options when possible

Part 2 – Lab norms that protect bodies (about 5 minutes)

Class culture can normalize stretch breaks and not mocking posture adjustments. Teachers/IT can rotate high-strain tasks.

Real-life scenario: Students laugh when someone asks to raise a monitor. The student stops asking and works in pain.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How should peers respond instead?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 3 – Equity and accommodation (about 5 minutes)

Not everyone thrives on the same chair or input device. Requesting accommodations is legitimate; gatekeeping “tough it out” is harmful.

Real-life scenario: A student with back pain is told ergonomic seating is “only for staff,” despite empty adjustable chairs in storage.

Critical thinking: What fair process should exist for student accommodations?

Do this:

  1. Write two sentences requesting a reasonable station change respectfully.
  2. Circle equitable stance: Match setup to need · Same painful setup for “fairness”

Part 4 – Remote/home inequality (about 5 minutes)

Homework on beds or floors is common when desks are scarce. Productivity advice that ignores housing realities can shame students unfairly.

Real-life scenario: A rubric docks “professional setup photos” for a student sharing a small room with siblings.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How can expectations adapt without abandoning health goals?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

ExpectationSensitive to context?
Suggest low-cost tips (books as risers) as optionsYes · No

Offer school lab time as an alternativeYes · No

Part 5 – Advocacy one-pager (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Create a one-pager for student council/IT: 3 ergonomic upgrades for the lab + why (health + learning).

Include cost-aware options (low/medium).

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I see ergonomics as a shared-space issue.
  • □ I support norms that protect bodies.
  • □ I respect accommodations.
  • □ I avoid shaming home setups without options.
  • □ I can advocate for practical upgrades.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
