## Part 2 – Lab norms that protect bodies (about 5 minutes)

Class culture can normalize stretch breaks and not mocking posture adjustments. Teachers/IT can rotate high-strain tasks.

**Real-life scenario:** Students laugh when someone asks to raise a monitor. The student stops asking and works in pain.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How should peers respond instead?

_________________________________________

|---|---|

| Allow water/stand breaks without penalty | Yes · No |

## Part 3 – Equity and accommodation (about 5 minutes)

Not everyone thrives on the same chair or input device. Requesting accommodations is legitimate; gatekeeping “tough it out” is harmful.

**Real-life scenario:** A student with back pain is told ergonomic seating is “only for staff,” despite empty adjustable chairs in storage.

**Critical thinking:** What fair process should exist for student accommodations?

## Part 4 – Remote/home inequality (about 5 minutes)

Homework on beds or floors is common when desks are scarce. Productivity advice that ignores housing realities can shame students unfairly.

**Real-life scenario:** A rubric docks “professional setup photos” for a student sharing a small room with siblings.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How can expectations adapt without abandoning health goals?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Yes** or **No**.

| Expectation | Sensitive to context? |
|---|---|
| Suggest low-cost tips (books as risers) as options | Yes · No |
| Require expensive chairs for full credit | Yes · No |
| Offer school lab time as an alternative | Yes · No |
