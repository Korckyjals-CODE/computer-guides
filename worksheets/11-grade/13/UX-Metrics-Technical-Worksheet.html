Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: UX metrics are numbers that describe user experience. Examples include task success rate, time on task, error rate, SUS score, NPS, and conversion rate.

Do this:

  1. Name one number that could show a website is easy to use.
  2. Circle a metric that measures how long a user takes: Time on task · Brand color

Part 2 – Performance metrics (about 5 minutes)

Task success rate = successes ÷ attempts × 100. Time on task measures speed. Error rate counts mistakes per attempt.

Real-life scenario: Of 20 users, 15 finish the task. Success rate = 15 ÷ 20 = 75%.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Is 75% good enough for a checkout page? Why?

Part 3 – Attitude metrics (about 5 minutes)

SUS (System Usability Scale) is a 10-question survey scored from 0 to 100. NPS asks how likely users are to recommend a product. CSAT measures satisfaction.

Real-life scenario: A SUS score of 68 is considered about average.

Critical thinking: Why should you combine numbers with comments?

Word bank: SUS · NPS · CSAT · Success rate

Do this: Choose the metric.

Question or dataMetric
10-question usability survey
“How likely would you recommend this?”
“How satisfied are you today?”

Part 4 – Choosing and reading metrics (about 5 minutes)

Pick metrics that match goals. A small sample or one metric alone can mislead. Look at trends, not one number.

Real-life scenario: Conversion rises, but the error rate also rises. Users complete purchases but make mistakes.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What does this mixed result suggest?

Do this:

  1. Write which two metrics you would track for a school-lunch ordering app.
  2. Circle the better practice: Combine metrics · Use one number only
