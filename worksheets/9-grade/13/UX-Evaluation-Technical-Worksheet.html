UX Evaluation: Technical Worksheet

Grade 9 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: UX evaluation is the process of checking how well a product works for real users. Evaluators use observation, surveys, heuristics, and metrics to find problems and decide on improvements.

Do this:

  1. Name one product you would like to evaluate and one question you would ask users.
  2. Circle the method that watches a user perform a task: Observation · Advertising

Part 2 – Evaluation methods (about 5 minutes)

Common methods: usability testing (watch users try tasks), surveys (ask opinions), heuristic evaluation (experts check rules of good design), and analytics (data about clicks and time).

Real-life scenario: A team watches 5 students use a library app and notes where each hesitates.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What can observation show that a survey might miss?

Do this: Match each method with its strength.

Part 3 – Nielsen heuristics (about 5 minutes)

Experts use heuristics, such as visibility of system status, error prevention, consistency, and help users recover from errors.

Real-life scenario: A site shows no progress bar during upload. Users do not know if it works.

Critical thinking: Which heuristic is broken here?

Word bank: Visibility of status · Consistency · Error prevention · Recovery

Do this: Choose the heuristic being broken.

ProblemHeuristic
Upload shows no progress
Buttons look different on every page
A delete has no undo

Part 4 – Reporting results (about 5 minutes)

A good report lists problems, severity (how bad), evidence, and a recommended fix.

Real-life scenario: The report says: “3 of 5 users could not find Search. Severity: high. Fix: move it to the top.”

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why include evidence, not just opinions?

Do this:

  1. Write one usability finding in this style: problem, evidence, fix.
  2. Circle the stronger finding: “4 of 5 users missed the button” · “I do not like it”

Part 5 – Evaluation sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a table with three problems, their severity (high/medium/low), and a fix.

Write which problem you would fix first and why.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.
