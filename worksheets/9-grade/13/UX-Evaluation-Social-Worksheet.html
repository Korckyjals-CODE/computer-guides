UX Evaluation: Social Worksheet

Grade 9 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: UX evaluation affects real people. Ethical evaluators get consent, protect privacy, include diverse participants, and report honestly, even when results are unwelcome.

Do this:

  1. Write one thing a researcher should ask before observing a person.
  2. Circle the ethical step: Ask for consent · Record secretly

Part 2 – Consent and privacy (about 5 minutes)

Participants should know what will be recorded, why, and how data is stored. They can stop at any time.

Real-life scenario: A researcher records screens. Before starting, she explains and asks permission.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could go wrong if a recording is shared without permission?

Do this: Circle Ethical or Not ethical.

Part 3 – Diverse participants (about 5 minutes)

Testing with a narrow group gives a narrow picture. Include different ages, abilities, languages, and devices.

Real-life scenario: A bank app is tested only by young adults. Older customers struggle.

Critical thinking: How could the team recruit a wider group?

Word bank: Older adults · People with disabilities · New users · Different languages

Do this: Match a group with what it can reveal.

Part 4 – Honest reporting (about 5 minutes)

Honest reports include negative results. Hiding problems harms users and the team's trust.

Real-life scenario: A manager asks to remove a bad result from the report.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should the evaluator do?

Do this:

  1. Write two reasons to report problems honestly.
  2. Circle the responsible action: Report all findings · Keep only good news

Part 5 – Ethics checklist (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a checklist of 4 ethical rules for evaluation.

Write which rule you think is most important and why.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know why consent matters.
  • □ I can explain the value of diverse participants.
  • □ I know to report honestly.
  • □ I can protect privacy.
  • □ I can list ethical rules.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
