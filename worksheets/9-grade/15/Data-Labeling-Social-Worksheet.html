Data Labeling: Social Worksheet

Grade 9 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Labels reflect human choices. People who label data may have bias, may be underpaid, and may see sensitive content, so labeling raises ethical questions.

Do this:

  1. Name one person who could be affected by a wrong label.
  2. Circle what fair labeling needs: Clear rules and respect for workers · Speed only

Part 2 – Bias in labels (about 5 minutes)

If labelers always tag certain jobs with one gender, the model learns stereotypes. Diverse teams and clear rules reduce this risk.

Real-life scenario: A hiring app learned from labels that favored one group.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Who might be harmed?

Part 3 – People behind the labels (about 5 minutes)

Many labelers work long hours for low pay. Some see disturbing content. Ethical companies provide fair pay and support.

Real-life scenario: A worker labels violent images all day without breaks.

Critical thinking: What support does she need?

Word bank: pay · breaks · support

Part 4 – Privacy in datasets (about 5 minutes)

Datasets may include faces or voices. Consent and anonymization protect the people in them.

Real-life scenario: A company uses classroom photos to train a face model without asking.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What is the problem?

Do this:

  1. Write two questions to ask before using photos in a dataset.
  2. Circle the ethical choice: Ask for consent · Collect quietly

Part 5 – Ethics checklist (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a checklist for fair data labeling.

Mark the item you think matters most.

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.
