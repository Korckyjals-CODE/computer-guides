Saving Work: Technical Worksheet

Grade 2 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Saving means keeping your work so it does not disappear. When you save, the computer stores your work in a file. You can open it later.

Do this:

  1. Write one kind of work you might want to save.
  2. Circle the button that keeps your work: Save · Erase

Part 2 – Why we save (about 5 minutes)

If the computer turns off before you save, your work can be lost. Saving protects it.

Real-life scenario: Carlos writes a story. The power goes out. He did not save.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should Carlos do next time?

Do this: Circle Save now or Wait.

MomentBest choice
You finished a drawingSave now · Wait
You wrote one line only and will stopSave now · Wait
Before turning the computer offSave now · Wait

Part 3 – Where to save (about 5 minutes)

Work can be saved in a folder on the computer or on a USB stick. A clear name helps you find it.

Real-life scenario: Ruby saves her homework in the folder Homework, in a file named Story-Ruby.

Critical thinking: Why are folders and names useful?

Word bank: Name · Folder · USB

Do this: Write the word that matches.

ClueWord
Small stick that carries files
Place for files
What you call your file

Part 4 – Save often (about 5 minutes)

Save early and save often. Many people press Ctrl + S as a shortcut.

Real-life scenario: Dan works for 20 minutes without saving. The computer freezes.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How often would you save?

Do this:

  1. Write the shortcut keys for Save.
  2. Circle the best habit: Save every few minutes · Save only at the end

Part 5 – My saving plan (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a computer, a folder, and an arrow showing where your work goes.

Write the name you would give to your work.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know why we save.
  • □ I know a folder keeps files.
  • □ I know a good name helps.
  • □ I know Ctrl + S.
  • □ I save my work often.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
