Taking Care of Devices: Social Worksheet

Grade 2 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Devices often belong to a family, a class, or a school. Taking care of them means asking before you use, being gentle when you borrow, and telling someone if something goes wrong. In this worksheet you will practice kind device habits with other people.

Do this:

  1. Name one shared device (home or school).
  2. Circle who else might use it: Family · Classmates · Both

Part 2 – Ask before you use (about 5 minutes)

A shared device is not only yours. Asking shows respect. Waiting your turn keeps people calm.

Real-life scenario: Camila's brother is doing homework on the family tablet. Camila grabs it to watch a video without asking. The homework file closes. Her brother feels upset.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could Camila say instead of grabbing?

Do this: Circle Respectful or Not respectful.

ActionRespectful or Not?
Ask, “May I use the tablet when you finish?”Respectful · Not respectful
Take a phone from a parent's handRespectful · Not respectful
Wait in line for the class laptop cartRespectful · Not respectful

Part 3 – Borrow with care (about 5 minutes)

When you borrow a device, treat it as carefully as if it were new. Return it charged enough to use, and in its place.

Real-life scenario: Diego borrows a classmate's headphones for listening time. He leaves them tangled on the floor. The next student trips on the cord.

Critical thinking: Write one rule for borrowing headphones or a tablet.

Do this: Number the steps 1, 2, and 3 for borrowing well.

StepOrder (1, 2, or 3)
Return the device to its place
Ask permission
Use it gently during your turn

Part 4 – If something goes wrong (about 5 minutes)

Devices can drop, crack, or stop working. Telling the truth helps adults fix problems. Hiding a problem can make it worse for everyone.

Real-life scenario: A class tablet slips from Sofia's hands onto a carpet. The screen still works, but a corner looks dented. Sofia is scared she will get in trouble.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should Sofia say to the teacher?

Do this: Circle the better choice.

SituationBetter choice
You spill a little water near a laptopTell a grown-up · Hide the spill
You notice a cracked screen you did not makeTell a grown-up · Pretend you did not see
A charger will not fit and feels stuckForce it in · Ask for help

Part 5 – A sharing care sign (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a sign for a shared device area. Write one short rule (ask, wait, return, tell).
  1. Write who the sign helps.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know many devices are shared with family or classmates.
  • □ I can ask before I use someone else's device.
  • □ I can name steps for borrowing with care.
  • □ I know to tell the truth if a device is hurt or wet.
  • □ I can make a short sign about sharing care.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
