## How to use this worksheet

- Do the parts **in order** from top to bottom.
- Each part has a **time** so you know how long to spend.
- Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

---

## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** When we share files, our **names** help others. A clear file name is a kind gesture for the person who opens it.

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one reason to give a file a clear name.

   _________________________

2. **Circle** the thoughtful choice: Clear name · No name

## Part 2 – Naming for others (about 5 minutes)

If you share a file, your friend must find it. A name like **Class Story** helps your friend know what it is.

**Real-life scenario:** Ava sends a file called “aaaa” to a friend. Her friend does not open it.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why might her friend ignore it?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Kind** or **Not kind**.

| Choice | Kind? |
|---|---|
| A clear name | Kind · Not kind |
| A secret name | Kind · Not kind |
| Name plus my name | Kind · Not kind |
| No name at all | Kind · Not kind |

## Part 3 – Working as a team (about 5 minutes)

A team can agree on how to name files. This is a **team rule**. Everyone follows it.

**Real-life scenario:** Three friends each name files in a different way. The folder is messy.

**Critical thinking:** How can the team fix this?

_________________________________________

**Word bank:** *Rule* · *Team* · *Same*

## Part 4 – Respect other people's files (about 5 minutes)

Do not change the name of a file that is not yours. Ask first.

**Real-life scenario:** Max renames a friend's drawing as a joke. The friend cannot find it.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What should Max do?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

1. **Write** what to say before changing a name.

   _________________________

2. **Circle** the respectful choice: Ask first · Rename it
