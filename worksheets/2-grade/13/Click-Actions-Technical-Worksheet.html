Click Actions: Technical Worksheet

Grade 2 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: A click is a quick press of a mouse button. Different clicks do different things: single click selects, double click opens, right click shows a menu, and drag moves things.

Do this:

  1. Write one thing on a computer you can open with a double click.
  2. Circle the click that usually opens a file: Single click · Double click

Part 2 – Types of clicks (about 5 minutes)

A single click selects an item. A double click usually opens it. A right click opens a small menu with choices.

Real-life scenario: Carlos single-clicks a folder and it turns blue. Then he double-clicks and it opens.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why do we need different kinds of clicks?

Do this: Match each action with the click. Circle the best click.

ActionClick type
Choose a pictureSingle · Double · Right
Open a folderSingle · Double · Right
See a menu to copySingle · Double · Right

Part 3 – Drag and drop (about 5 minutes)

To drag, hold the left button down, move the mouse, then let go to drop. You can drag a file into a folder.

Real-life scenario: Ruby drags her homework file into the folder named Homework.

Critical thinking: What can go wrong if you let go of the button too early?

Word bank: Drag · Drop · Hold

Do this: Write the step that matches.

StepWord
Press and keep pressing the button
Move the mouse with the item
Let go of the button

Part 4 – Click practice (on paper) (about 5 minutes)

Practice makes clicks easier. Use slow, gentle clicks. If nothing happens, wait a moment before clicking again.

Real-life scenario: Dan clicks five times quickly and opens five windows by accident.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should Dan do instead?

Do this:

  1. Draw the symbol for a single click (one tap) and a double click (two taps).
  2. Circle the best habit: Click once, then wait · Click many times fast

Part 5 – My click map (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a mouse. Label the left button, right button, and wheel. Add an arrow showing a drag.

Write what you do when a click does not work.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know a single click selects.
  • □ I know a double click opens.
  • □ I know a right click shows a menu.
  • □ I can explain drag and drop.
  • □ I click slowly and gently.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
