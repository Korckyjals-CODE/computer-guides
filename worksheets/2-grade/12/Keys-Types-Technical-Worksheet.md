## Part 2 – Letter keys (about 5 minutes)

**Letter keys** are the keys that type the alphabet (A, B, C … Z). Most of the middle of a keyboard is letter keys. You use them to write words and names.

**Real-life scenario:** Mia wants to type her name. She finds the **M** key, then the **I** key, then the **A** key. Those are letter keys.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why do we need many letter keys on a keyboard?

## Part 3 – Number keys (about 5 minutes)

**Number keys** type the digits **0–9**. Many keyboards have a row of number keys above the letter keys. You use them for ages, dates, scores, and phone numbers.

**Real-life scenario:** Sam writes the date for today's worksheet. He presses **1**, then **2**. Those are number keys.

**Critical thinking:** Can you write the number twelve with letter keys only? Why or why not?

## Part 4 – Special keys (about 5 minutes)

**Special keys** do jobs that are not typing a letter or a number. Common ones:

- **Space bar** — makes a blank space between words  
- **Enter** (or Return) — starts a new line  
- **Backspace** — erases the last thing you typed  
- **Shift** — helps make a capital letter when you hold it and press a letter key  

**Real-life scenario:** Leo typed “ilike cats” with no spaces. He puts the cursor between “i” and “like”, then taps the **space bar**. Now it reads “i like cats”.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Which special key would you use if you typed the wrong letter by mistake?
