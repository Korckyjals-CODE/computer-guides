Preventive Care: Social Worksheet

Grade 7 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Preventive care is also a shared responsibility. Labs, families, and teams succeed when people report early warnings, respect update times, and avoid “someone else will fix it.” In this worksheet you will examine habits, peer pressure, and fair lab rules.

Do this:

  1. Name a shared tech space you use (lab, cart, home computer).
  2. Write one habit that helps everyone there—not only you.

Part 2 – Shared labs and early reporting (about 5 minutes)

When many people use the same machines, small problems grow if nobody speaks up. Early reporting is preventive: a loose key today can become a broken keyboard tomorrow.

Real-life scenario: Three students notice a laptop battery drains in 20 minutes. Each thinks, “The next class will deal with it.” By Friday the device dies mid-presentation.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What social barrier stops students from reporting small issues? How could a simple report form or sticky-note board help?

Part 3 – Peer pressure and ignored warnings (about 5 minutes)

Friends may joke that updates are “annoying” or that backups are “for nerds.” Ignoring warnings to look casual can put shared work at risk.

Real-life scenario: During club time, a dialog asks to restart for an update. Someone says, “Skip it—we only have 15 minutes.” The group skips. Next meeting the app crashes and the project file corrupts.

Critical thinking: Write one respectful sentence you could say to defend taking the update (or saving and restarting).


Do this: Choose the wiser group choice.

Part 4 – Fair rules and downtime (about 5 minutes)

Preventive care sometimes needs planned downtime (maintenance windows). Fair rules explain when devices will be offline, who is responsible, and how students get notified.

Real-life scenario: IT shuts the lab for dust cleaning and cable checks during lunch with no notice. Students arrive with unfinished homework and feel the school “doesn't care.”

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How could notice + a backup workspace make preventive care feel fairer?

Do this: Write two fair-rule ideas for a shared lab.

Part 5 – Community care pledge (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Design a poster or pledge card with three group habits for preventive care (report, update, backup, leave tidy).
  1. Explain who benefits if the pledge is followed.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I see preventive care as a shared responsibility, not only an IT job.
  • □ I can explain why early reporting helps a group.
  • □ I can resist peer pressure to skip updates or backups.
  • □ I can suggest fair rules around maintenance downtime.
  • □ I can draft a short community care pledge.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
