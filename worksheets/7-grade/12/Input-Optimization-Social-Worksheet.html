Input Optimization: Social Worksheet

Grade 7 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Optimizing input is not only speed—it must stay inclusive, respectful of privacy, and fair to people who type slowly, use assistive tech, or share devices.

Do this:

  1. Name a classmate need that could affect input (language, motor skill, vision, shared device…).
  2. Circle a social risk: Forcing one “fast” method for everyone · Offering alternatives

Part 2 – Inclusive options (about 5 minutes)

Voice input, larger targets, keyboard-only navigation, and clear labels help more people. Optimization that excludes is incomplete.

Real-life scenario: A timed quiz only accepts lightning mouse clicks on tiny buttons. A student using keyboard navigation cannot finish.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What alternative input should the quiz offer?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 3 – Privacy in autofill (about 5 minutes)

Saved passwords and autofill are convenient but risky on shared school devices. Optimization must not leak personal data.

Real-life scenario: A student saves a personal email password on a lab PC. The next period’s user sees suggested logins.

Critical thinking: What school rule would balance convenience and privacy?

Do this:

  1. Write one habit for shared computers regarding autofill.
  2. Circle safer practice: Sign out + skip saving passwords on lab PCs · Save everything for speed

Part 4 – Pressure vs support (about 5 minutes)

Teams sometimes “optimize” by shaming slower typists. Better social optimization teaches shortcuts and shares load fairly.

Real-life scenario: During paired coding, one partner mocks the other’s typing speed instead of offering to alternate roles.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How can roles optimize output without humiliation?

Part 5 – Inclusive optimization brief (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Write/draw a one-page brief: optimize a class check-in form for speed AND inclusion (list 3 features).

Star the feature that protects privacy or accessibility most.

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.
