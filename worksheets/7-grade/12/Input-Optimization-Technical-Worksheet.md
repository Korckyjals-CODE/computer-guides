## Part 2 – Match method to task (about 5 minutes)

Not every task fits every input. Long essays favor keyboards; quick choices may favor buttons; drawing favors stylus/touch. Optimization starts by matching **method → task**.

**Real-life scenario:** A survey with 40 open text boxes frustrates users who only needed yes/no choices. Redesigning as options reduces typing.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** When is a multiple-choice input more optimized than free typing?

## Part 3 – Reduce repeated entry (about 5 minutes)

Optimization removes repeat work: **templates**, autocomplete, copy-paste with care, saved profiles, and defaults. Enter once, reuse safely.

**Real-life scenario:** Every lab report, students retype the school header. A template with the header already filled saves minutes each time.

**Critical thinking:** What risk appears if autocomplete fills the wrong saved address?

## Part 4 – Error-proofing input (about 5 minutes)

Good input design **validates** early: required fields, format hints (YYYY-MM-DD), confirm-before-delete. Catching errors at entry is cheaper than fixing later.

**Real-life scenario:** A form accepts letters in a phone field. Later the calling system fails. A digit-only field would have blocked the mistake.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Name one confirmation that is worth the extra click.
