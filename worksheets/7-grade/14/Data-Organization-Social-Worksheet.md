## How to use this worksheet

- Do the parts **in order** from top to bottom.
- Each part has a **time** so you know how long to spend.
- Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

---

## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** Organized data should be fair and respectful. People deserve accurate records, and private data must be protected.

## Part 2 – Privacy (about 5 minutes)

Some data, like addresses and grades, is **private**. Only the right people should see it.

**Real-life scenario:** A teacher leaves a grade list open on a shared screen.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What could go wrong?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Protect** or **Share**.

| Data | Protect or share? |
|---|---|
| Home address | Protect · Share |
| Class schedule on a public board | Protect · Share |
| Grades of one student | Protect · Share |
| Favorite color shared by choice | Protect · Share |

## Part 3 – Accuracy (about 5 minutes)

Wrong data can hurt people, for example a misspelled name on a certificate.

**Real-life scenario:** A student's name is wrong on the award list.

**Critical thinking:** How should it be corrected?

## Part 4 – Shared responsibility (about 5 minutes)

Everyone who handles data must follow rules and ask before sharing.

**Real-life scenario:** Two students copy a classmate's data without asking.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** How should they act?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one rule for handling classmates' data.

   _________________________

2. **Circle** the responsible choice: Ask permission · Copy quietly
