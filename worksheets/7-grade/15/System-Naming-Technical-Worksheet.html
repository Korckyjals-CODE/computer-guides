Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: In a computer system, names identify files, folders, drives, users, and devices. Operating systems apply rules to names: maximum length, forbidden characters, and whether capital letters matter.

Do this:

  1. Name two kinds of things in a computer that need a name.
  2. Circle what the system uses to find a resource: Its name and path · Its color

Part 2 – Names and paths (about 5 minutes)

A path shows where a file lives: C:/School/Math/Report-1.docx. The path is made of folder names and the file name. Changing a folder name changes the path.

Real-life scenario: A student renames the folder Math to Maths. A shortcut to Report-1 stops working.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why did the shortcut break?

Part 3 – Rules in different systems (about 5 minutes)

Systems differ. Windows ignores upper and lower case, many Linux systems do not, and each limits characters. Names like CON or a/b may be blocked.

Real-life scenario: Report.docx and report.docx can exist together in Linux but not in Windows.

Critical thinking: What problem does this cause when moving files?

Part 4 – Naming users and devices (about 5 minutes)

Systems also name users (login names), devices (LAPTOP-02) and networks (Wi-Fi names). A clear naming plan avoids duplicates and makes troubleshooting easier.

Real-life scenario: Two lab computers have the same device name. The network confuses them.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: How can a naming plan help?

Do this:

  1. Write a pattern for naming five lab computers.
  2. Circle the better device name: LAB1-PC05 · my computer
