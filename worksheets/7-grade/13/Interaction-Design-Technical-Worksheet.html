Interaction Design: Technical Worksheet

Grade 7 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Interaction design is planning how a person and a digital product communicate: what the user does (input) and how the product responds (feedback).

Do this:

  1. Name one device you use daily and one action you take on it.
  2. Circle the part of design that shows a result after you act: Feedback · Input only

Part 2 – Input and feedback (about 5 minutes)

Every interaction has an action (tap, click, type) and a response (a sound, a color change, a message). Clear feedback tells users that the system understood them.

Real-life scenario: Dana taps Send. The button turns gray and shows “Sending…” then “Sent ✓”.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What if there were no feedback after tapping Send?

Do this: Circle Feedback or Not feedback.

ResponseType

Part 3 – Affordances and signifiers (about 5 minutes)

An affordance is what an object lets you do. A signifier is a clue that shows it, like a raised button or an underline on a link.

Real-life scenario: A flat gray label looks like text. A shaded rectangle looks like a button.

Critical thinking: How do signifiers prevent confusion?

Word bank: Button shape · Underline · Arrow icon · Slider

Do this: Choose the clue that fits.

ActionSignifier
Click a link
Press to submit
Drag to change volume

Part 4 – Error prevention (about 5 minutes)

Good design prevents mistakes with confirmations, undo, and clear labels.

Real-life scenario: A message asks “Delete this file? Yes / No” before removing it.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: When is a confirmation helpful, and when can it be annoying?

Do this:

  1. Write one way a form can prevent errors.
  2. Circle the better design: Warn before deleting · Delete instantly with no undo

Part 5 – Interaction sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a screen of a simple app with a button and a feedback message after it is pressed.

Write one improvement for the interaction.
