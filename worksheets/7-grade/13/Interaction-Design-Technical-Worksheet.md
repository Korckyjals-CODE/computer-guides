## Part 2 – Input and feedback (about 5 minutes)

Every interaction has an **action** (tap, click, type) and a **response** (a sound, a color change, a message). Clear feedback tells users that the system understood them.

**Real-life scenario:** Dana taps Send. The button turns gray and shows “Sending…” then “Sent ✓”.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What if there were no feedback after tapping Send?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle **Feedback** or **Not feedback**.

| Response | Type |
|---|---|
| Button changes color when pressed | Feedback · Not feedback |
| The screen stays blank | Feedback · Not feedback |
| A loading bar appears | Feedback · Not feedback |
| A “Saved” message | Feedback · Not feedback |

## Part 3 – Affordances and signifiers (about 5 minutes)

An **affordance** is what an object lets you do. A **signifier** is a clue that shows it, like a raised button or an underline on a link.

**Real-life scenario:** A flat gray label looks like text. A shaded rectangle looks like a button.

**Critical thinking:** How do signifiers prevent confusion?

## Part 4 – Error prevention (about 5 minutes)

Good design prevents mistakes with **confirmations**, **undo**, and **clear labels**.

**Real-life scenario:** A message asks “Delete this file? Yes / No” before removing it.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** When is a confirmation helpful, and when can it be annoying?
