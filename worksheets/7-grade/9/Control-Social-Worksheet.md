## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

## Part 2 – Who sets the goal (about 5 minutes)

The set point looks technical. It is also a decision about whose comfort counts. If one office sets the only thermostat, every other room lives with that choice. People in a hotter room are not “using the system wrong.” They never received the control.

**Real-life scenario:** The school thermostat is locked. The main office is comfortable at the chosen set point. A classroom on the sunny side is much warmer. Students ask to change it. The panel says only a building account can adjust the goal.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Whose comfort became the set point? What would a fairer way to share that control look like?

## Part 3 – A way to pause the rule (about 5 minutes)

An **override** is a deliberate pause or change by a person when the automatic rule no longer fits. A system with no override treats every exception as a mistake. Safety rules can stay firm. Comfort and timing rules usually need a human pause.

**Real-life scenario:** Hallway lights use a motion sensor and turn off quickly. A class sits on the floor for a reading circle. The lights go out because no one is walking. There is no switch the teacher can hold on for the next 20 minutes.

**Critical thinking:** Should this light system have an override? Write yes or no, and say who should be allowed to use it. Then name one control you would not let students override, and why.

## Part 4 – Strict control, changed situation (about 5 minutes)

Automatic rules are often sold as fair because they treat everyone the same. Sameness is not fairness when people did not have the same conditions. A timer that ignores a disrupted class punishes students for a building event.

**Real-life scenario:** A quiz locks when the timer hits zero. A fire drill uses 15 of the 30 minutes. The system still locks. Students who were outside cannot finish. The dashboard shows a high number of “incomplete” scores.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What goal was the timer controlling? What did that goal fail to measure?
