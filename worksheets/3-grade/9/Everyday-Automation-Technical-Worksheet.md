## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** **Automation** means a machine follows a set of **steps** after something starts it. A person does not do every step. In this worksheet you will learn what a **trigger** is, how the steps repeat, and how a simple automatic machine has a start, a job, and a stop.

**Do this:**

1. **Have you used a machine that keeps working after you press one button?** Circle: Yes or No

2. **Name one machine** at home or at school that does a job by itself.

## Part 2 – What starts the machine (about 5 minutes)

A **trigger** is what starts the machine. It can be a button, a timer, or something the machine notices, like a person walking near a door. **Steps** are the actions the machine does in order. On a washing machine, the trigger is the start button. The steps are fill, wash, rinse, and spin. The machine stops when the last step is done.

**Real-life scenario:** You put a slice of bread in a toaster and push the lever down. The toaster heats the bread. Then it pops the bread up. You did not hold the bread over the heat.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What is the trigger on the toaster? What is one step the toaster does without you?

## Part 3 – The same steps, again and again (about 5 minutes)

**Repeat** means the machine does the same steps the next time you trigger it. It does not forget the order and it does not get tired. A person sorting crayons may slow down. A machine that sorts blocks can do the same sort every time, as long as it is working.

**Real-life scenario:** A library has a machine that drops returned books into bins, one book after another. Each book follows the same path.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why is repeating the same steps useful for a boring job? Name one job you would still want a person to do.

## Part 4 – Start, job, and stop (about 5 minutes)

A simple automatic machine has three ideas. The **trigger** starts it. The **job** is the work it does. The **stop** is how it knows the work is finished. A stop can be a beep, a door that closes, or a timer that reaches zero.

**Critical thinking:** What could go wrong if a machine has a job but no stop? Write one sentence.
