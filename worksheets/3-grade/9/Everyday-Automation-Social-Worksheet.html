Everyday Automation: Social Worksheet

Grade 3 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Automatic machines can save time, but people still make the choices. In this worksheet you will think about who a machine helps, which jobs should stay with people, how to stay safe, and how to be fair when a machine is shared.

Do this:

  1. Name one chore a machine does in your home or school.
  2. Circle who that machine helps most: Me · My family or class · Both

Part 2 – Who the machine helps (about 5 minutes)

A machine can help one person a lot and still leave work for someone else. Help is not the same for every person in the room. Someone may still need to load it, watch it, or reach the buttons.

Real-life scenario: The dishwasher ran overnight, so the plates are clean at breakfast. A younger child cannot reach the buttons. A grown-up still has to start the next load.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Who saved time in this family? Who still has a job to do?

Do this: Fill in the table.

Machine Who it helps One job a person still does
Washing machine
Automatic pencil sharpener
Sliding door at school

Part 3 – Staying safe around machines (about 5 minutes)

Automatic machines move, get hot, or close by themselves. A safe rule tells people when to wait, when to ask a grown-up, and when the machine should pause.

Real-life scenario: An automatic door at school starts to close. A student with a rolling backpack is still in the doorway.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should the door do? What should people nearby do?

Do this: Circle the safer choice on each line.

Situation Safer choice (circle one)
An automatic door sees someone in the way Close right away · Wait until the way is clear
A toaster is hot A child uses it alone · A grown-up stays nearby
A washing machine is running Climb on it · Keep hands and toys outside

Part 4 – Sharing one machine (about 5 minutes)

When many people need the same machine, the group needs a fair rule. A fair rule is clear, and it does not let one person keep the machine the whole time if others are waiting.

Real-life scenario: The class has one automatic pencil sharpener. Three students need it at the same time. One student sharpens every pencil in a full box while the others wait.

Critical thinking: Write one fair rule for the sharpener. Your rule should say who goes next or how long one person may use it.

Do this: Number these actions 1, 2, and 3 in an order that is fair. 1 happens first.

Action Order (1, 2, or 3)
The next student in line uses the sharpener
Students who need a sharp pencil line up
The first student steps aside after one or two pencils

Part 5 – A sign people can follow (about 5 minutes)

People use a shared machine more kindly when the rule is easy to see. A short sign can remind them to wait, take turns, or ask for help.

Do this:

  1. Draw a sign for one machine at school (a door, a sharpener, or a machine you choose). Write the words of the rule on the sign. You may add colors.
  1. Write one sentence. Who is your sign trying to help?

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can name who an automatic machine helps and who still has a job.
  • □ I can choose a safer way to be near a machine that moves or gets hot.
  • □ I can write a fair rule for a machine the class shares.
  • □ I know a machine can save time and still need a person.
  • □ I can make a simple sign that tells people how to use a machine kindly.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
