## Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

**What you will learn:** Not everyone can use a mouse the same way. People have different hands, vision, and tools. **Accessible** technology helps everyone click and type with precision.

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one reason someone might find a small button hard to click.

2. **Circle** the idea that is true: Tools can fit different people · Everyone needs the same settings

## Part 2 – Different hands, different needs (about 5 minutes)

Some people have shaking hands, a small hand, or only one hand. Others see small things with difficulty. Technology offers choices for all.

**Real-life scenario:** Pablo has a tremor. A large pointer and slow speed help him click.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** Why should settings change to fit the person?

## Part 3 – Accessibility tools (about 5 minutes)

**Accessibility** means everyone can use technology. Tools include a big pointer, **zoom**, sticky keys, and voice control.

**Real-life scenario:** A student with low vision turns on high contrast and zoom to read the screen.

**Critical thinking:** Who benefits when a tool is easy to use?

_________________________________________

**Word bank:** *Zoom* · *Large pointer* · *Voice control*

**Do this:** Match a person with a good tool.

## Part 4 – Being kind (about 5 minutes)

We respect everyone's pace. We never laugh at someone who needs help or more time.

**Real-life scenario:** A classmate takes a long time to click. Some students sigh.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What could you say to be supportive?

_________________________________________

**Do this:**

1. **Write** one kind sentence for someone who needs more time.

   _________________________
