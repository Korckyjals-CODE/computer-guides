Precision: Technical Worksheet

Grade 3 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Precision means doing something exactly, not just roughly. With a mouse, precision means putting the pointer on the right spot and clicking exactly what you want.

Do this:

  1. Write one task where small mistakes matter (for example, clicking a tiny button).
  2. Circle what precision means: Exact · Roughly

Part 2 – Small targets (about 5 minutes)

Small buttons and links are hard to click. We slow down and use small hand movements to be precise.

Real-life scenario: Nora tries to click a tiny close (X) button. She slows down and hits it on the first try.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why does slowing down help with small targets?

Do this: Circle the easier target.

Part 3 – Tools that help precision (about 5 minutes)

Computers offer helps: zoom makes things bigger, arrow keys move a little at a time, and mouse speed settings change how far the pointer goes.

Real-life scenario: Omar zooms to 200% so he can place a shape exactly on the line.

Critical thinking: Which help would you choose to place a tiny picture exactly?

Word bank: Zoom · Arrow keys · Mouse speed · Grid

Do this: Choose the best help.

ProblemTool
Text is too small to click
Move a shape one tiny step
The pointer is too fast

Part 4 – Checking your work (about 5 minutes)

Precision also means checking. Look at the result. Did you click the right item? Undo (Ctrl+Z) fixes mistakes.

Real-life scenario: Ivy selects the wrong file. She sees it, presses Ctrl+Z, and tries again.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What can you do after a mistake?

Do this:

  1. List two ways to check your work.
  2. Circle the habit that supports precision: Look before you click · Click first, look later

Part 5 – Precision sketch (about 5 minutes)

Do this: Draw a mouse pointer on a tiny button. Show how zoom makes it larger.

Write one thing you will do this week to be more precise.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know precision means exact.
  • □ I can use zoom or slower movements.
  • □ I can name a tool that helps (arrow keys, mouse speed).
  • □ I know to check my work.
  • □ I know undo fixes mistakes.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
