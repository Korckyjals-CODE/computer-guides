Typing Logic: Technical Worksheet

Grade 3 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Typing logic means using a plan when you type: home-row idea, spaces between words, capitals with Shift, and fixing mistakes with Backspace—so text is clear and readable.

Do this:

  1. Name one thing you type at school (your name, a sentence, a list…).
  2. Circle a goal of typing logic: Clear readable text · Typing with eyes closed for fun only · Drawing pictures

Part 2 – Spaces and word order (about 5 minutes)

Words need spaces between them. Letters inside a word stay together. Typing logic says: finish a word → space → next word.

Real-life scenario: Tara types “Ilovemy dog” as one blob, then “I love my dog” with spaces. The second line is easy to read.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why does a missing space confuse a reader?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 3 – Capitals with Shift (about 5 minutes)

A capital letter often starts a sentence or a name. Hold Shift and press a letter key at the same time. That is typing logic for capitals—not Caps Lock for every letter.

Real-life scenario: Ben wants “Maria”. He holds Shift + M, then types aria without Shift.

Critical thinking: What is wrong with leaving Caps Lock on for a whole sentence of names and normal words?

Do this:

  1. Write one name that needs a capital letter.
  2. Circle the better habit: Shift for one capital · Caps Lock for every letter always

Part 4 – Fix mistakes in order (about 5 minutes)

When you see a typo, move to it (or use Backspace from the end) and fix one mistake at a time. Random mashing of keys creates more errors.

Real-life scenario: Lila typed “freind”. She uses Backspace to erase d-n-i-e-r-f and retypes “friend” carefully.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: Why is slow careful fixing better than slamming many keys?

Do this: Circle Yes or No.

Part 5 – Plan a short sentence (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Plan a short sentence (4–6 words). Write it here first.
  2. List the steps you will use: capitals, spaces, check spelling.

Optional: Draw the keys you need for the first word of your sentence (simple boxes are fine).

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know typing logic means having a plan when I type.
  • □ I put spaces between words.
  • □ I can use Shift for a capital letter.
  • □ I fix mistakes carefully, one at a time.
  • □ I can plan a short sentence before typing.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
