Safe Use: Technical Worksheet

Grade 3 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Safe use means using a device in ways that protect you, your information, and the device. You will learn body position, password care, and what to click—or not click.

Do this:

  1. Name one device you use at school or home.
  2. Circle one safe-use idea you already know: Sit well · Keep passwords private · Ask before clicking

Part 2 – Sit and hold safely (about 5 minutes)

Your body needs care at a computer. Sit so your feet can rest. Keep the screen at a comfortable height. Hold tablets with both hands when you can. Do not eat or drink over the keyboard.

Real-life scenario: Maya works with a laptop on the edge of a desk. Her juice cup sits next to the keys. When she reaches for her pencil, the cup tips toward the laptop.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could Maya change to make this safer?

Do this: Write Safe or Not safe.

ActionSafe or Not safe?
Sit with the screen at eye level
Rest a drink on the keyboard
Carry a tablet with two hands
Balance a laptop on a wobbly chair arm

Part 3 – Protect your login (about 5 minutes)

A password (or PIN) is a secret key to your account. Do not share it. Do not write it on a sticky note stuck to the screen. Log out when you finish on a shared computer.

Real-life scenario: Leo tells a friend his school password so they can “help” finish a game. Later, the friend logs in as Leo and changes Leo’s avatar without asking.

Critical thinking: Why can sharing a password cause problems even with a friend?

Do this: Match each idea with a word from the word bank.

Part 4 – Think before you click (about 5 minutes)

Pop-ups, strange links, and “free prize” buttons can be traps. Safe use means pausing. Ask a trusted adult if something looks odd. Stay on sites and apps your school or family allows.

Real-life scenario: While reading a school page, a bright window jumps up: “You won a phone! Click NOW!” The address bar looks different from the school site.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What should you do first—click or ask for help?

Do this: Circle the safer choice.

SituationSafer choice
A pop-up says you won a prizeClick · Ask an adult
You need a site for class workUse the school list · Type a random site
A download appears without askingCancel / ask · Install it

Part 5 – Draw a safe-use checklist (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw yourself using a device safely. Label three safe steps (sit well, private password, think before click).
  1. Write one sentence about the step you will remember most.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I can explain what safe use means for a device.
  • □ I know safe ways to sit and hold a device.
  • □ I know a password should stay private and I should log out.
  • □ I can pause and ask before clicking odd pop-ups or links.
  • □ I can show three safe-use steps in a drawing or list.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
