## Part 2 – Sit and hold safely (about 5 minutes)

Your body needs care at a computer. Sit so your feet can rest. Keep the screen at a comfortable height. Hold tablets with both hands when you can. Do not eat or drink over the keyboard.

**Real-life scenario:** Maya works with a laptop on the edge of a desk. Her juice cup sits next to the keys. When she reaches for her pencil, the cup tips toward the laptop.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What could Maya change to make this safer?

## Part 3 – Protect your login (about 5 minutes)

A **password** (or PIN) is a secret key to your account. Do not share it. Do not write it on a sticky note stuck to the screen. Log out when you finish on a shared computer.

**Real-life scenario:** Leo tells a friend his school password so they can “help” finish a game. Later, the friend logs in as Leo and changes Leo’s avatar without asking.

**Critical thinking:** Why can sharing a password cause problems even with a friend?

## Part 4 – Think before you click (about 5 minutes)

Pop-ups, strange links, and “free prize” buttons can be traps. **Safe use** means pausing. Ask a trusted adult if something looks odd. Stay on sites and apps your school or family allows.

**Real-life scenario:** While reading a school page, a bright window jumps up: “You won a phone! Click NOW!” The address bar looks different from the school site.

**Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing:** What should you do first—click or ask for help?

_________________________________________

**Do this:** Circle the safer choice.

| Situation | Safer choice |
|-----------|--------------|
| A pop-up says you won a prize | Click · Ask an adult |
| You need a site for class work | Use the school list · Type a random site |
| A download appears without asking | Cancel / ask · Install it |
