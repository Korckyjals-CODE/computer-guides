Safe Use: Social Worksheet

Grade 3 · Worksheet · About 25 minutes
Work with pen and paper. Use color pencils if you like. You do not need a computer or the internet.

Name: Grade/Section: Date:
How to use this worksheet
  • Do the parts in order from top to bottom.
  • Each part has a time so you know how long to spend.
  • Read every instruction before you write. You can do this on your own.

Part 1 – What you will learn (about 3 minutes)

What you will learn: Safe use is also about people. We share devices, take turns, speak kindly, and help the group stay safe. In this worksheet you will practice fairness and responsibility with technology.

Do this:

  1. Name one shared device in your class or at home.
  2. Circle who else might use it: Classmates · Family · Both

Part 2 – Fair turns keep everyone safe (about 5 minutes)

Rushing or grabbing can cause drops and hard feelings. Fair turns mean waiting, asking, and leaving the device ready for the next person.

Real-life scenario: Two students both want the last tablet. One pulls it from the other’s hands. The tablet falls. Both feel upset, and class time is lost.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could each student say to share fairly?

Do this: Mark Helps the group or Hurts the group.

ActionHelps or Hurts?
Wait in line for the tablet cart
Grab a tablet from someone’s hands
Return the device plugged in when done
Keep a shared tablet past your turn on purpose

Part 3 – Kind words around technology (about 5 minutes)

How we talk about people online and offline matters. Do not post or share photos of classmates without permission. Do not tease someone about a mistake on a screen.

Real-life scenario: Ana misspells a word on the class board app. Another student laughs loudly and says her mistake to the whole room. Ana wants to stop trying.

Critical thinking: Write one kind thing a classmate could say instead.

Do this: Circle Kind or Not kind.

ActionKind or Not kind?
Ask before taking a classmate’s photoKind · Not kind
Share a funny video of someone without askingKind · Not kind
Help a partner fix a typing error quietlyKind · Not kind

Part 4 – Speak up for safety (about 5 minutes)

If you see a spill, a cracked screen, a mean message, or a scary pop-up, tell a trusted adult. Speaking up protects the whole group. You are not “tattling”—you are helping.

Real-life scenario: Sam sees water near a keyboard. Sam did not spill it. Sam worries that telling will make someone angry.

Discuss with a partner or reflect in writing: What could Sam say to the teacher?

Do this: Number the helpful steps 1, 2, and 3.

StepOrder (1, 2, or 3)
Ask an adult for help
Stay calm and do not touch the spill or pop-up
Notice something unsafe

Part 5 – A class safe-use sign (about 5 minutes)

Do this:

  1. Draw a class sign with one short safe-use rule (take turns, be kind, tell an adult).
  1. Write who the sign helps.

Part 6 – Check what you learned (about 2 minutes)

Checklist. Put a ✓ in the box when it is true for you.

  • □ I know safe use includes fair turns with shared devices.
  • □ I can choose kind words and ask before sharing photos.
  • □ I know to tell a trusted adult about spills, cracks, or scary messages.
  • □ I can name actions that help or hurt the group.
  • □ I can make a short safe-use sign for the class.

When all boxes are checked, you have reviewed the main ideas from this worksheet.
